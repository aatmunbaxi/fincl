1 Getting Started

Acquire the dependencies for fincl and the library itself and load it. Assuming you have registered the package locally to quicklisp, you can load the library

(ql:quickload :fincl)
(defpackage #:fun-in-fincl (:use #:cl #:fincl))
(in-package #:fun-in-fincl)

;;; Literals for dates and tenors
(named-readtables:in-readtable fincl:syntax)

The closest thing to a "Hello, World!" of a quantitative finance library is pricing a European option with the Black-Scholes formula.

(defconstant +today+ #D"2026-10-01")
(defparameter *market*
  (make-bs-market :valuation-date +today+
                  :spot 100 :rate 0.04d0
                  :dividend 0.01d0
                  :vol 0.2d0))
(defparameter *option*
  (make-option (call 110) (expires #T"1Y" :from +today+)))
(price *option* (make-engine :black-scholes) *market*)
;; ==>  5.240727867335534d0
;;      NIL
;;     #<ENGINE (make-engine :black-scholes)>

2 Options

An option is contract giving the holder the right, but not the obligation, to exercise a purchase or sale of an underlying asset at an agreed-upon price. Options have finite lifetime, and carry various exercise rights. e.g. European options can be exercised on the expiration date, while American options can be exercised at any point before the expiration.

Besides exercise rights, the primary difference of option types is how their payoff is assessed. The major supported option payoff schemes are presented in this section. The user may write their own option payoff schemes (see TODO).

Throughout, $K$ denotes the strike price of the option. $t\in [0, T]$ denotes a time between the inception of the option $0$ and the expiration date $T$ (measured in years), and $S_t$ denotes the price of the underlying asset at time $t$. $\phi$ will $\pm 1$ for calls/puts, respectively.

← ↑ → ↺

2.1 Vanilla Options

$$\max(\phi (S_{T} - K) , 0).$$

Exercise is European unless the option says otherwise (see make-option).

  • [class] FINCL:VANILLA-PAYOFF FINCL:STRIKE-PAYOFF

    max(phi (S - K), 0). Engines with closed forms specialize on this class.

    (make-instance 'vanilla-payoff :kind :put :strike 40d0)

← ↑ → ↺

2.2 Asian Options

With $A$ the average of $S_t$ over the observation dates $t_1,\dots,t_n$ (arithmetic $\frac{1}{n}\sum_i S _{t_i}$ or geometric $(\prod_i S _{t_i})^{1/n}$):

  • fixed strike: $\max(\phi (A - K), 0)$

  • floating strike: $\max(\phi (S_T - A), 0)$

European exercise only.

  • [class] FINCL:ASIAN FINCL:OBSERVED-PATH

    The payoff is applied to the average of the observable on DATES (arithmetic or geometric, equal weights). A :FIXED strike pays the payoff of the average; a :FLOATING strike pays max(phi (S_T - A), 0).

← ↑ → ↺

2.3 Barrier Options

The vanilla payoff, conditional on the path touching a level $H$ before $T$, monitored continuously or on a list of dates. With rebate $R$:

  • knock-out: the payoff if $H$ is never touched; $R$, paid at the touch, otherwise

  • knock-in: the payoff if $H$ is touched; $R$, paid at $T$, otherwise

The type is one of :down-and-out, :down-and-in, :up-and-out, :up-and-in. European exercise only.

  • [class] FINCL:BARRIER FINCL:OBSERVED-PATH

    A single barrier at LEVEL. A knock-out pays nothing once the observable has touched it and pays REBATE when it does; a knock-in pays only if it has, and REBATE at expiry otherwise. Monitored continuously, or on DATES.

← ↑ → ↺

2.4 Lookback Options

With $m = \min_t S_t$ and $M = \max_t S_t$ over the observation dates (every simulation step by default):

  • floating strike: call $S_T - m$, put $M - S_T$

  • fixed strike: call $\max(M - K, 0)$, put $\max(K - m, 0)$

European exercise only.

  • [class] FINCL:LOOKBACK FINCL:OBSERVED-PATH

    The extremum of the observable: a :FLOATING strike pays phi (S_T - extremum), the minimum for a call and the maximum for a put; a :FIXED strike pays the payoff of the maximum (call) or minimum (put).

← ↑ → ↺

2.5 make-option

(make-option clause...) builds an option from clauses, which may come in any order. Arguments of a clause are evaluated.

  • payoff: (call K) or (put K); one is required

  • exercise: (european) (default), (american), or (bermudan :dates dates) / (bermudan :every tenor :from date)

  • (expires date), or (expires tenor :from date); required, except that bermudan :dates takes the last date

  • path feature, optional: (average :arithmetic :dates dates) (or :geometric, :every tenor :from date, :strike-type :floating), (barrier :down-and-out level :rebate R :monitoring dates) (continuous by default), (lookback :floating) (or :fixed)

  • (underlying name) and (currency name), when the market has several

(make-option (put 95) (american) (expires #T"1Y" :from +today+))
(make-option (call 100) (barrier :up-and-out 130 :rebate 2) (expires #D"2027-06-15"))
(make-option (call 100) (average :arithmetic :every #T"1M" :from +today+)
             (expires #T"1Y" :from +today+))

make-option-from-clauses takes the same clauses as a list, for building options from data.

  • [macro] FINCL:MAKE-OPTION &REST CLAUSES

    Build an option from clauses: a kind (call/put) and strike or a payoff scheme, an expiry, and optionally exercise rights. With no payoff clause the payoff is vanilla; with no exercise clause the option is European.

    A vanilla European call K=110 with 1Y expiry (make-option (call 110) (expiry #T"1Y" :from +today+)) Clause arguments are evaluated.

  • [function] FINCL:MAKE-OPTION-FROM-CLAUSES CLAUSES

    Build an option from clauses: a kind (call/put) and strike or a payoff scheme, an expiry, and optionally exercise rights. With no payoff clause the payoff is vanilla; with no exercise clause the option is European.

    A vanilla European call K=110 with 1Y expiry (make-option (call 110) (expiry #T"1Y" :from +today+)) CLAUSES is a list of (head . arguments), as data.

3 Pricing engines

(price option engine market) returns (values npv standard-error engine). An engine is an instance, a keyword designator, a list (:cos :n-terms 512), a process (priced by the first engine that can), or :auto, which tries the engines below in order of speed and accuracy and falls back to Monte Carlo with a warning. Build one with make-engine, which evaluates its arguments.

(make-engine :cos :process (make-heston) :n-terms 512)
(price option :auto market)

Designators:

  • :black-scholes: closed forms; European, barriers, geometric Asians

  • :baw: American

  • :merton-series: European, Merton jump diffusion

  • :cev: European, CEV with $\beta\le 1$

  • :binomial: American, Bermudan, GBM

  • :cos: European, Bermudan, American; any process with a characteristic function

  • :fd: European, Bermudan, American, barriers; GBM, CEV, local vol, Heston

  • :mc: any exercise and process that can be simulated

  • [function] FINCL:MAKE-ENGINE KEY &REST SETTINGS

    The engine designated by KEY (:black-scholes, :baw, :binomial, :cos, :mc, :merton-series, :cev, or any class's (:designator ...)) with SETTINGS, its initargs, evaluated. :PROCESS goes to engines that take one; an engine whose model is fixed (Black-Scholes, BAW) accepts a process only of its model, with no parameters set (it prices with the market's data).

    (make-engine :binomial :n-steps 4000) (make-engine :cos :process (make-heston :xi 0.5d0) :n-terms 512)

← ↑ → ↺

3.1 Black-Scholes

Closed forms in the forward, the discount factor and the total variance to expiry, so they hold under term structures of rates, dividends and vol. European vanillas; continuous barriers (Reiner-Rubinstein) and discrete monitoring through the continuity correction of Broadie, Glasserman and Kou; discrete geometric Asians.

See F. Black and M. Scholes, The pricing of options and corporate liabilities, Journal of Political Economy 81 (1973); E. Reiner and M. Rubinstein, Breaking down the barriers, Risk 4 (1991); M. Broadie, P. Glasserman and S. Kou, A continuity correction for discrete barrier options, Mathematical Finance 7 (1997); A. Kemna and A. Vorst, A pricing method for options based on average asset values, Journal of Banking and Finance 14 (1990).

  • [class] FINCL:BLACK-SCHOLES-ENGINE FINCL:ANALYTIC-ENGINE

    Black's formula for European vanillas.

← ↑ → ↺

3.2 Barone-Adesi-Whaley

Quadratic approximation of the American premium over the European price. Returns the European price where early exercise is never optimal.

See G. Barone-Adesi and R. E. Whaley, Efficient analytic approximation of American option values, Journal of Finance 42 (1987).

  • [class] FINCL:BARONE-ADESI-WHALEY-ENGINE FINCL:ANALYTIC-ENGINE

    Quadratic approximation for American vanillas.

← ↑ → ↺

3.3 Merton series

European vanillas under Merton jump diffusion as a Poisson-weighted sum of Black-Scholes prices conditional on the number of jumps.

See R. C. Merton, Option pricing when underlying stock returns are discontinuous, Journal of Financial Economics 3 (1976).

  • [class] FINCL:MERTON-SERIES-ENGINE FINCL:ANALYTIC-ENGINE

    Merton's (1976) series for European vanillas under a jump diffusion: Black prices conditional on the number of jumps, weighted by their Poisson probabilities.

    (make-instance 'merton-series-engine :process (make-instance 'merton-jump :intensity 1))

← ↑ → ↺

3.4 CEV formula

European vanillas under CEV with $\beta\le 1$ and absorption at zero, in terms of the noncentral $\chi^2$ distribution.

See R. Schroder, Computing the constant elasticity of variance option pricing formula, Journal of Finance 44 (1989).

  • [class] FINCL:CEV-ENGINE FINCL:ANALYTIC-ENGINE

    Schroder's (1989) noncentral chi-square formula for European vanillas under CEV with absorption at zero, beta <= 1.

    (make-instance 'cev-engine :process (make-instance 'cev :beta 0.5d0))

← ↑ → ↺

3.5 Binomial tree

Cox-Ross-Rubinstein tree for GBM. By default the last step is valued with the Black-Scholes formula to remove the odd-even oscillation (BBS), and the price is $2v(N)-v(N/2)$ (BBSR). Bermudan dates snap to the nearest step.

  • n-steps: steps in the tree

  • smooth: Black-Scholes on the last step

  • extrapolate: Richardson extrapolation

See J. C. Cox, S. A. Ross and M. Rubinstein, Option pricing: a simplified approach, Journal of Financial Economics 7 (1979); M. Broadie and J. Detemple, American option valuation: new bounds, approximations, and a comparison of existing methods, Review of Financial Studies 9 (1996).

  • [class] FINCL:BINOMIAL-ENGINE FINCL:ENGINE

    Cox-Ross-Rubinstein binomial tree.

    (make-instance 'binomial-engine :n-steps 5000)

← ↑ → ↺

3.6 COS

Fourier-cosine expansion of the density of the log-return, with coefficients from the characteristic function. Europeans need only the characteristic function; Bermudans and Americans use the backward recursion, which needs state-independent log-increments, with the American price extrapolated from Bermudans of $M, 2M, 4M, 8M$ dates.

  • n-terms: terms in the expansion

  • truncation: half-width $L$ of the truncation range, in cumulant scale

  • richardson-dates: $M$

See F. Fang and C. W. Oosterlee, A novel pricing method for European options based on Fourier-cosine series expansions, SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 31 (2008), and Pricing early-exercise and discrete barrier options by Fourier-cosine series expansions, Numerische Mathematik 114 (2009).

  • [class] FINCL:COS-ENGINE FINCL:ENGINE

    Fang and Oosterlee's Fourier-cosine (COS) method. Europeans need only a characteristic function; Bermudans and Americans use the backward recursion of their 2009 paper, which needs a process whose log-increments are independent of the state (the INDEPENDENT-INCREMENTS mixin; GBM).

    (make-instance 'cos-engine :process (make-instance 'heston) :n-terms 160)

← ↑ → ↺

3.7 Finite differences

Crank-Nicolson on a log-spot grid concentrated at the strike, with Rannacher start-up after each payoff discontinuity. Americans and Bermudans by Ikonen-Toivanen; barriers by truncating the grid or by events on the monitoring dates. Heston by Hundsdorfer-Verwer ADI on a (log-spot, variance) grid. Jump processes are refused.

  • n-space, n-variance, n-time: grid sizes

  • theta: time-stepping weight, $\frac{1}{2}$ is Crank-Nicolson

  • rannacher-steps: implicit substeps after a discontinuity

  • concentration, width: grid concentration and half-width in standard deviations

See R. Rannacher, Finite element solution of diffusion problems with irregular data, Numerische Mathematik 43 (1984); S. Ikonen and J. Toivanen, Operator splitting methods for American option pricing, Applied Mathematics Letters 17 (2004); K. J. in 't Hout and S. Foulon, ADI finite difference schemes for option pricing in the Heston model with correlation, International Journal of Numerical Analysis and Modeling 7 (2010).

  • [class] FINCL:FD-ENGINE FINCL:ENGINE

    Finite differences in log-spot: a theta-scheme with Rannacher start-up on a sinh-concentrated grid, Ikonen-Toivanen splitting for American exercise, continuous barriers as boundary conditions; Heston by Hundsdorfer-Verwer ADI.

    (make-engine :fd :process (make-local-vol) :n-space 400 :n-time 400)

← ↑ → ↺

3.8 Monte Carlo

Simulates the paths of any process that defines a stepper. Terminal-only payoffs sample the terminal value exactly where the process allows; the rest step through a time grid containing every observation date. Americans and Bermudans by least-squares regression on the continuation value. Barriers monitored continuously are corrected with the Brownian bridge. Prices do not depend on the number of threads or chunks.

  • process, n-paths, n-steps, seed

  • rng: :counter (counter-based Philox), :sobol (randomized quasi-Monte Carlo) or a random-state generator

  • antithetic: antithetic pairs

  • control-variates: :european, :geometric-asian

See P. Glasserman, Monte Carlo Methods in Financial Engineering (2004); F. A. Longstaff and E. S. Schwartz, Valuing American options by simulation: a simple least-squares approach, Review of Financial Studies 14 (2001); J. K. Salmon, M. A. Moraes, R. O. Dror and D. E. Shaw, Parallel random numbers: as easy as 1, 2, 3, Proceedings of SC11 (2011); S. Joe and F. Y. Kuo, Constructing Sobol sequences with better two-dimensional projections, SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 30 (2008).

  • [class] FINCL:MONTE-CARLO-ENGINE FINCL:ENGINE

4 Stochastic processes

A process is a stochastic differential equation. For the purposes of financial modeling, these are typically drift-diffusion processes or Lévy processes. However, fincl allows you to define your own (see TODO).

A general drift-diffusion process has the form $$dS_t = \mu(X_t, t) + \sigma(X_t,t)dW_t,$$ where $\mu(X_t,t)$ is called the drift, and $\sigma(X_t,t)$ is the diffusion. $W_t$ is a standard Brownian motion.

The Lévy processes add a jump term to drift-diffusion models dictated by a Poisson process. All processes forms are given under the risk-netural measure, where $r$ is the risk-free rate, $q$ is an equity dividend.

← ↑ → ↺

4.1 Drift-diffusion processes

← ↑ → ↺

4.1.1 Geometric Brownian motion

The classical drift-diffusion model of constant volatility. $$dS_t = (r - q) S_t \,dt + \sigma S_t\,dW_t.$$

  • [class] FINCL:GBM FINCL:STOCHASTIC-PROCESS FINCL:INDEPENDENT-INCREMENTS

    Black-Scholes dynamics, with the market's rate, dividend and (by default) vol term structures.

  • [function] FINCL:MAKE-GBM &REST INITARGS &KEY VOL

    A new gbm process: (make-instance 'gbm initarg...).

← ↑ → ↺

4.1.2 Constant elasticity of variance

$$dS_t = (r-q)\,S_t\,dt + \sigma S_t^{\beta}\,dW_t$$

  • $\beta$: elasticity of the diffusion in $S$

  • $\sigma$: scale of the diffusion

See J. C. Cox, The constant elasticity of variance option pricing model (1975).

  • [class] FINCL:CEV FINCL:STOCHASTIC-PROCESS

    Constant-elasticity-of-variance dynamics.

  • [function] FINCL:MAKE-CEV &REST INITARGS &KEY BETA SIGMA SCHEME

    A new cev process: (make-instance 'cev initarg...).

← ↑ → ↺

4.1.3 Dupire local volatility

$$\frac{dS_t}{S_t} = (r-q)\,dt + \sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}(S_t,t)\,dW_t$$

where $\sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}$ is the local volatility of an implied total variance surface $w(k,T)$, $k =\ln (K / F (T) )$, in Gatheral's form of Dupire's formula,

$$\sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}^2 =\frac{\partial _T w}{1 - \frac{k}{w}\partial _k w + \frac{1}{4}\left(-\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{w} + \frac{k^2}{w^2}\right)(\partial _k w)^2+ \frac{1}{2}\partial _{kk} w}.$$

The surface is the market's unless one is given.

  • [class] FINCL:LOCAL-VOL FINCL:STOCHASTIC-PROCESS

    Dupire local volatility consistent with an implied-vol surface: Monte Carlo prices of Europeans reproduce the surface.

  • [function] FINCL:MAKE-LOCAL-VOL &REST INITARGS &KEY SURFACE SUBSTEPS

    A new local-vol process: (make-instance 'local-vol initarg...).

← ↑ → ↺

4.1.4 Heston

  • [class] FINCL:HESTON FINCL:STOCHASTIC-PROCESS

    Heston stochastic volatility model.

  • [function] FINCL:MAKE-HESTON &REST INITARGS &KEY V0 KAPPA THETA XI RHO SCHEME

    A new heston process: (make-instance 'heston initarg...).

  • [generic-function] FINCL:MAKE-STEPPER PROCESS MARKET TIMES

    Dispatch once on (process, market) and return a closure

    (lambda (state normals j) ...)

    that advances STATE in place from TIMEs[j] to TIMEs[j+1], consuming PROCESS-FACTORS normals. TIMEs is a (SIMPLE-ARRAY DOUBLE-FLOAT (*)) of grid times in market time, starting at 0 and possibly unevenly spaced. The closure is called once per path per step, so everything that can be hoisted belongs in the method body: per-interval drifts, variances and step lengths are computed there into typed vectors indexed by J. Rates enter through LOG-GROWTH over each interval, so the discounted observable is a martingale under any deterministic term structure. A process that is not resolved (see RESOLVE-PROCESS) is resolved at the last grid time.

← ↑ → ↺

4.1.5 NGARCH

$$\ln S_t = \ln S _{t-1} + (r-q)\Delta - \frac{1}{2} h _t + \sqrt{h _t}\,z _t, \qquad h _{t+1} = \omega + \beta h _t + \alpha\, h _t\,(z _t-\gamma )^2$$

with $z_t$ standard normal and period $\Delta$.

  • $\omega$: intercept of the variance

  • $\beta$: persistence of the variance

  • $\alpha$: sensitivity of the variance to shocks

  • $\gamma$: leverage, raising the next variance more after negative shocks

See J.-C. Duan, The GARCH option pricing model, Mathematical Finance 5 (1995).

  • [class] FINCL:GARCH FINCL:STOCHASTIC-PROCESS

    Duan's NGARCH(1,1).

  • [function] FINCL:MAKE-GARCH &REST INITARGS &KEY PERIOD ALPHA BETA GAMMA OMEGA H0

    A new garch process: (make-instance 'garch initarg...).

← ↑ → ↺

4.1.6 Hull-White

$$dr_t = \left(\theta(t) - a\,r_t\right)dt + \sigma\,dW_t$$

  • $a$: speed of mean reversion

  • $\sigma$: volatility of the short rate

  • $\theta(t)$: fitted to the initial yield curve

See J. Hull and A. White, Pricing interest-rate derivative securities, Review of Financial Studies 3 (1990).

  • [class] FINCL:HULL-WHITE FINCL:STOCHASTIC-PROCESS

    Hull-White one-factor short-rate model, fitted to a yield curve. Priced through rates engines (:JAMSHIDIAN, :HULL-WHITE-TREE); it does not drive an equity spot.

  • [function] FINCL:MAKE-HULL-WHITE &REST INITARGS &KEY A SIGMA

    A new hull-white process: (make-instance 'hull-white initarg...).

← ↑ → ↺

4.2 Lévy processes

Lévy processes add jump terms to drift-diffusion models, accounting for random jumps seen in the price movement of various instruments. Throughout, $dN_t$ will denote a Poisson process with rate $\lambda$.

← ↑ → ↺

4.2.1 Merton Jump Diffusion

$$\frac{dS_t}{S_t} = (r-q-\lambda k)\,dt + \sigma\,dW_t + (e^{Y}-1)\,dN_t, \qquad Y \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_J, \sigma_J^2),$$

with $k = e^{\mu_J + \sigma_J^2/2} - 1$.

  • $\sigma$: volatility of the diffusion

  • $\lambda$: jump intensity

  • $\mu_J$, $\sigma_J$: mean and standard deviation of the log jump $Y$

See R. C. Merton, Option pricing when underlying stock returns are discontinuous, Journal of Financial Economics 3 (1976).

  • [class] FINCL:MERTON-JUMP FINCL:JUMP-DIFFUSION

    Merton's jump diffusion: GBM plus normally distributed log jumps at Poisson times.

  • [function] FINCL:MAKE-MERTON-JUMP &REST INITARGS &KEY VOL INTENSITY JUMP-MEAN JUMP-VOL

    A new merton-jump process: (make-instance 'merton-jump initarg...).

← ↑ → ↺

4.2.2 Kou Jump Diffusion

Kou's jump-diffusion process dictated by $$\frac{dS_t}{S_t} = (r-q-\lambda)\, dt + \sigma \,dW_t + (e^{Y_t} - 1)\,dN_t.$$ See S.G. Kou, "A jump-diffusion model for option pricing"

  • [class] FINCL:KOU-JUMP FINCL:JUMP-DIFFUSION

    Kou's jump diffusion: GBM plus double-exponential log jumps at Poisson times.

← ↑ → ↺

4.2.3 Variance Gamma

$$S_t = S_0\exp\left((r-q+\omega)\,t + \theta \, G_t + \sigma \, W_{G_t}\right), \qquad \omega = \frac1\nu\ln\left(1 - \theta\nu - \tfrac12\sigma^2\nu\right),$$

where $G$ is a gamma process with $\mathbb{E}[G_t] = t$ and $\operatorname{Var}[G_t] = \nu t$.

  • $\theta$: drift of the Brownian motion, setting the skewness

  • $\sigma$: volatility of the Brownian motion

  • $\nu$: variance rate of the gamma clock, setting the kurtosis

See D. B. Madan, P. P. Carr and E. C. Chang, The variance gamma process and option pricing, European Finance Review 2 (1998).

  • [class] FINCL:VARIANCE-GAMMA FINCL:EXPONENTIAL-LEVY FINCL:STOCHASTIC-PROCESS

    The variance gamma process of Madan, Carr and Chang.

  • [function] FINCL:MAKE-VARIANCE-GAMMA &REST INITARGS &KEY SIGMA NU THETA

    A new variance-gamma process: (make-instance 'variance-gamma initarg...).

← ↑ → ↺

4.2.4 Normal Inverse Gaussian

$$S_t = S_0\exp\left((r-q+\omega)\,t + \beta V_t + W_{V_t}\right), \qquad \omega = \delta\left(\sqrt{\alpha^2-(\beta+1)^2} - \gamma\right),$$

where $V$ is an inverse Gaussian process with $\mathbb{E}[V_t] = \delta t/\gamma$ and $\gamma = \sqrt{\alpha^2-\beta^2}$.

  • $\alpha$: heaviness of the tails, lighter as it grows

  • $\beta$: asymmetry, with $|\beta| < \alpha$

  • $\delta$: scale

See O. E. Barndorff-Nielsen, Processes of normal inverse Gaussian type, Finance and Stochastics 2 (1998).

  • [class] FINCL:NIG FINCL:EXPONENTIAL-LEVY FINCL:STOCHASTIC-PROCESS

    Barndorff-Nielsen's normal inverse Gaussian process.

  • [function] FINCL:MAKE-NIG &REST INITARGS &KEY ALPHA BETA DELTA

    A new nig process: (make-instance 'nig initarg...).

← ↑ → ↺

4.2.5 CGMY

$$S_t = S_0\exp\left((r-q+\omega)\,t + L_t\right), \qquad \omega = -C\,\Gamma(-Y)\left((M-1)^Y - M^Y + (G+1)^Y - G^Y\right),$$

where $L$ is a pure-jump process with Lévy density

$$\nu(x) = C\,\frac{e^{-G|x|}}{|x|^{1+Y}}\ (x<0), \qquad \nu(x) = C\,\frac{e^{-Mx}}{x^{1+Y}}\ (x>0).$$

  • $C$: overall activity

  • $G$, $M$: exponential decay of the left and right tails, with $M > 1$

  • $Y < 2$: fine structure of the jumps

See P. Carr, H. Geman, D. B. Madan and M. Yor, The fine structure of asset returns, Journal of Business 75 (2002).

  • [class] FINCL:CGMY FINCL:EXPONENTIAL-LEVY FINCL:STOCHASTIC-PROCESS

    The CGMY tempered stable process.

  • [function] FINCL:MAKE-CGMY &REST INITARGS &KEY C G M Y

    A new cgmy process: (make-instance 'cgmy initarg...).

← ↑ ↺

4.2.6 Bates

$$\frac{dS_t}{S_t} = (r-q-\lambda k)\,dt + \sqrt{v_t}\,dW^1_t + (e^{Y}-1)\,dN_t, \qquad dv_t = \kappa(\theta - v_t)\,dt + \xi\sqrt{v_t}\,dW^2_t,$$

with $\mathrm{corr}(dW^1_t, dW^2_t) = \rho$, $Y \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_J, \sigma_J^2)$ and $k = e^{\mu_J + \sigma_J^2/2} - 1$.

  • $v_0$, $\theta$: initial and long-run variance

  • $\kappa$: speed of mean reversion of the variance

  • $\xi$: volatility of the variance

  • $\rho$: correlation of the two Brownian motions

  • $\lambda$, $\mu_J$, $\sigma_J$: jump intensity, and mean and standard deviation of the log jump $Y$

See D. S. Bates, Jumps and stochastic volatility: exchange rate processes implicit in Deutsche mark options, Review of Financial Studies 9 (1996).
