1 Getting Started

Acquire the dependencies for fincl and the library itself and load it. Assuming you have registered the package locally to quicklisp, you can load the library

(ql:quickload :fincl)
(defpackage #:fun-in-fincl (:use #:cl #:fincl))
(in-package #:fun-in-fincl)

;;; Literals for dates and tenors
(named-readtables:in-readtable fincl:syntax)

The closest thing to a "Hello, World!" of a quantitative finance library is pricing a European option with the Black-Scholes formula.

(defconstant +today+ #D"2026-10-01")
(defparameter *market*
  (make-bs-market :valuation-date +today+
                  :spot 100 :rate 0.04d0
                  :dividend 0.01d0
                  :vol 0.2d0))
(defparameter *option*
  (make-option (call 110) (expires #T"1Y" :from +today+)))
(price *option* (make-engine :black-scholes) *market*)
;; ==>  5.240727867335534d0
;;      NIL
;;     #<ENGINE (make-engine :black-scholes)>

2 Dates and markets

Dates and tenors are read with #D"2026-10-01" and #T"3M" in the fincl:syntax readtable; date+ and date< move and compare dates.

A market holds the spots, curves, dividends and volatilities that engines read. It has a valuation date, there is no hidden "today", and it is immutable: a scenario is a new market (see bump). make-market builds one with several underlyings and curves from lists; make-bs-market builds the common case of one underlying, one currency and one curve, with flat inputs. Discrete cash and proportional dividends are given as a dividend-schedule, a list of (ex-date amount [:kind :cash|:proportional] [:pay-date date]).

(make-market :valuation-date #D"2026-10-01"
             :underlyings '((:spx :spot 5000 :currency :usd
                             :dividend 0.015d0 :vol 0.18d0))
             :curves '((:usd-ois :rate 0.04d0))
             :discounting '((:usd . :usd-ois)))

  • [class] FINCL:DATE STRUCTURE-OBJECT

    A calendar date. Immutable. Two dates are EQUALP when they are the same day, so they can key an EQUALP hash table.

    (make-date 2027 6 15) => #D"2027-06-15"

  • [class] FINCL:TENOR STRUCTURE-OBJECT

    A length of time in whole days, weeks, months or years. Immutable; two tenors are EQUALP when they have the same N and UNIT.

    (make-tenor 3 :months) => #T"3M"

  • [function] FINCL:DATE+ DATE DAYS

    The date DAYS calendar days after DATE (before, if DAYS is negative).

    (date+ #D"2027-06-15" 10) => #D"2027-06-25"

  • [function] FINCL:DATE< A B

  • [class] FINCL:MARKET

    State of the world at a valuation date.

  • [function] FINCL:MAKE-MARKET &KEY VALUATION-DATE (DAY-COUNTER FINCL:+ACTUAL-365-FIXED+) UNDERLYINGS CURVES DISCOUNTING FIXINGS

    A COMPOSITE-MARKET observed on VALUATION-DATE. The arguments are lists:

    UNDERLYINGS ((name :spot s [:currency c][:dividend q] [:dividend-schedule d][:vol v]) ...) S a positive real; C a keyword (default :DEFAULT); Q a real continuous dividend yield or a DIVIDEND-CURVE (default 0); D discrete dividends alongside Q, a DIVIDEND-SCHEDULE or the entries MAKE-DIVIDEND-SCHEDULE takes, ((ex-date amount [:kind k][:pay-date p]) ...); V a real Black vol or a BLACK-VOL-STRUCTURE (default none, for markets that need no vol). CURVES ((id :rate r) ...) or ((id :curve c) ...): R a real zero rate, C a YIELD-CURVE. DISCOUNTING ((currency . curve-id) ...): the curve each currency discounts on. If omitted and there is one curve, every currency discounts on it. FIXINGS ((index-id (date rate) ...) ...): past fixings of interest-rate indexes, DATE a date or a "YYYY-MM-DD" string.

    Reals are coerced to double-float.

    (make-market :valuation-date #D"2026-09-24" :underlyings '((:spx :spot 5000 :currency :usd :dividend 0.015d0 :vol 0.18d0) (:aapl :spot 230 :currency :usd :vol 0.25d0 :dividend-schedule (("2027-02-10" 0.26d0) ("2027-05-12" 0.26d0)))) :curves '((:usd-ois :rate 0.04d0)) :discounting '((:usd . :usd-ois)))

  • [function] FINCL:MAKE-BS-MARKET &KEY VALUATION-DATE (DAY-COUNTER FINCL:+ACTUAL-365-FIXED+) SPOT RATE (DIVIDEND 0.0d0) DIVIDEND-SCHEDULE VOL

    A flat Black-Scholes market: a COMPOSITE-MARKET with one underlying and one currency, both :DEFAULT, discounting on the curve :DEFAULT. Numeric inputs are coerced to double-float; pass double-float or rational literals, since 0.05 reads as a single-float and (float 0.05 1d0) is 0.05000000074505806d0. DIVIDEND-SCHEDULE adds discrete dividends, as in MAKE-MARKET.

    (make-bs-market :valuation-date #D"2026-09-24" :spot 100 :rate 0.05d0 :vol 0.2d0 :dividend-schedule '(("2027-03-15" 2)))

  • [generic-function] FINCL:VALUATION-DATE MARKET

    The date the market is observed on.

  • [class] FINCL:DIVIDEND-SCHEDULE FINCL:TERM-STRUCTURE

    Known discrete dividends of one underlying. Made by MAKE-DIVIDEND-SCHEDULE.

  • [function] FINCL:MAKE-DIVIDEND-SCHEDULE ENTRIES

    A DIVIDEND-SCHEDULE from ENTRIES, a list of

    (ex-date amount &key (kind :cash) pay-date)

    EX-DATE and PAY-DATE are dates or "YYYY-MM-DD" strings; PAY-DATE (default EX-DATE) may not precede EX-DATE. A :CASH AMOUNT is a non-negative sum of money, a :PROPORTIONAL one a fraction in [0, 1) of the price.

    (make-dividend-schedule '((#D"2027-02-10" 0.82d0 :pay-date #D"2027-03-01") (#D"2027-08-10" 0.85d0)))

  • [function] FINCL:BUMP MARKET FACTOR SHIFT

    A new market with the risk FACTOR moved by SHIFT; MARKET is unchanged. FACTOR is (:spot u), (:vol u), (:dividend u) (the continuous yield) or (:dividend-schedule u) (every discrete dividend amount) for underlying U, (:dividend-schedule u ex-date) for the dividends going ex on one date, (:curve id) for a yield curve, or (:quote id selector) for one input quote of a bootstrapped curve, chosen by its helper's tenor or index, the curve then rebootstrapped. SHIFT is (:relative x), multiplying by 1 + x, or (:absolute x), adding x: to the spot, the vol, the yield, the dividend amounts (floored at 0), the rate or the quoted rate.

    (bump m '(:spot :spx) '(:relative 1/100)) ; SPX spot up 1% (bump m '(:curve :usd-ois) '(:absolute 1/10000)) ; rates up 1bp (bump m (:quote :usd-sofr ,#T"5Y") '(:absolute 1/10000)) ; the 5Y quote up 1bp (bump m(:dividend-schedule :aapl ,#D"2027-02-10") '(:absolute 1/100))

3 Options

An option is contract giving the holder the right, but not the obligation, to exercise a purchase or sale of an underlying asset at an agreed-upon price. Options have finite lifetime, and carry various exercise rights. e.g. European options can be exercised on the expiration date, while American options can be exercised at any point before the expiration.

Besides exercise rights, the primary difference of option types is how their payoff is assessed. The major supported option payoff schemes are presented in this section. The user may write their own option payoff schemes (see TODO).

Throughout, $K$ denotes the strike price of the option. $t\in [0, T]$ denotes a time between the inception of the option $0$ and the expiration date $T$ (measured in years), and $S_t$ denotes the price of the underlying asset at time $t$. $\phi$ will $\pm 1$ for calls/puts, respectively.

  • [class] FINCL:OPTION FINCL:INSTRUMENT

  • [generic-function] FINCL:OPTION-PAYOFF OBJECT

  • [class] FINCL:PAYOFF

    What a contract pays as a function of the observable. Carries no slots: call/put and the option's K belong to the option payoffs in options.lisp, since a coupon or a swap leg has neither. Subclasses use COMPONENT-CLASS too, so construction languages can fill their slots from facts (:FACT slot option).

  • [generic-function] FINCL:PAYOFF-VALUE PAYOFF X

    Dispatching payoff. Convenient; too slow to call per path.

3.1 Vanilla Options

$$\max(\phi (S_{T} - K) , 0).$$

Exercise is European unless the option says otherwise (see make-option).

  • [class] FINCL:VANILLA-PAYOFF FINCL:STRIKE-PAYOFF

    max(phi (S - K), 0). Engines with closed forms specialize on this class.

    (make-instance 'vanilla-payoff :kind :put :strike 40d0)

← ↑ → ↺

3.2 Asian Options

With $A$ the average of $S_t$ over the observation dates $t_1,\dots,t_n$ (arithmetic $\frac{1}{n}\sum_i S _{t_i}$ or geometric $(\prod_i S _{t_i})^{1/n}$):

  • fixed strike: $\max(\phi (A - K), 0)$

  • floating strike: $\max(\phi (S_T - A), 0)$

European exercise only.

  • [class] FINCL:ASIAN FINCL:OBSERVED-PATH

    The payoff is applied to the average of the observable on DATES (arithmetic or geometric, equal weights). A :FIXED strike pays the payoff of the average; a :FLOATING strike pays max(phi (S_T - A), 0).

← ↑ → ↺

3.3 Barrier Options

The vanilla payoff, conditional on the path touching a level $H$ before $T$, monitored continuously or on a list of dates. With rebate $R$:

  • knock-out: the payoff if $H$ is never touched; $R$, paid at the touch, otherwise

  • knock-in: the payoff if $H$ is touched; $R$, paid at $T$, otherwise

The type is one of :down-and-out, :down-and-in, :up-and-out, :up-and-in. European exercise only.

  • [class] FINCL:BARRIER FINCL:OBSERVED-PATH

    A single barrier at LEVEL. A knock-out pays nothing once the observable has touched it and pays REBATE when it does; a knock-in pays only if it has, and REBATE at expiry otherwise. Monitored continuously, or on DATES.

← ↑ → ↺

3.4 Lookback Options

With $m = \min_t S_t$ and $M = \max_t S_t$ over the observation dates (every simulation step by default):

  • floating strike: call $S_T - m$, put $M - S_T$

  • fixed strike: call $\max(M - K, 0)$, put $\max(K - m, 0)$

European exercise only.

  • [class] FINCL:LOOKBACK FINCL:OBSERVED-PATH

    The extremum of the observable: a :FLOATING strike pays phi (S_T - extremum), the minimum for a call and the maximum for a put; a :FIXED strike pays the payoff of the maximum (call) or minimum (put).

3.5 make-option

(make-option clause...) builds an option from clauses, which may come in any order. Arguments of a clause are evaluated.

  • payoff: (call K) or (put K); one is required

  • exercise: (european) (default), (american), or (bermudan :dates dates) / (bermudan :every tenor :from date)

  • (expires date), or (expires tenor :from date); required, except that bermudan :dates takes the last date

  • path feature, optional: (average :arithmetic :dates dates) (or :geometric, :every tenor :from date, :strike-type :floating), (barrier :down-and-out level :rebate R :monitoring dates) (continuous by default), (lookback :floating) (or :fixed)

  • (underlying name) and (currency name), when the market has several

(make-option (put 95) (american) (expires #T"1Y" :from +today+))
(make-option (call 100) (barrier :up-and-out 130 :rebate 2) (expires #D"2027-06-15"))
(make-option (call 100) (average :arithmetic :every #T"1M" :from +today+)
             (expires #T"1Y" :from +today+))

make-option-from-clauses takes the same clauses as a list, for building options from data.

  • [macro] FINCL:MAKE-OPTION &REST CLAUSES

    Build an option from clauses: a kind (call/put) and strike or a payoff scheme, an expiry, and optionally exercise rights. With no payoff clause the payoff is vanilla; with no exercise clause the option is European.

    A vanilla European call K=110 with 1Y expiry (make-option (call 110) (expiry #T"1Y" :from +today+)) Clause arguments are evaluated.

  • [function] FINCL:MAKE-OPTION-FROM-CLAUSES CLAUSES

    Build an option from clauses: a kind (call/put) and strike or a payoff scheme, an expiry, and optionally exercise rights. With no payoff clause the payoff is vanilla; with no exercise clause the option is European.

    A vanilla European call K=110 with 1Y expiry (make-option (call 110) (expiry #T"1Y" :from +today+)) CLAUSES is a list of (head . arguments), as data.

4 Pricing engines

(price instrument engine market) returns (values npv standard-error engine). An instrument is an instance of an instrument: an option, bond, a swap. An engine is an instance, a keyword designator, a list (:cos :n-terms 512), a process (priced by the first engine that can), or :auto, which tries the engines below in order of speed and accuracy and falls back to Monte Carlo with a warning. Build one with make-engine, which evaluates its arguments.

(make-engine :cos :process (make-heston) :n-terms 512)
(price option :auto market)

Designators:

  • :black-scholes: closed forms; European, barriers, geometric Asians

  • :baw: American

  • :merton-series: European, Merton jump diffusion

  • :cev: European, CEV with $\beta\le 1$

  • :binomial: American, Bermudan, GBM

  • :cos: European, Bermudan, American; any process with a characteristic function

  • :fd: European, Bermudan, American, barriers; GBM, CEV, local vol, Heston

  • :mc: any exercise and process that can be simulated

← ↑ → ↺

4.1 Pricing

Price an instrument with an engine under a market. price returns the value, its standard error (NIL for deterministic engines) and the engine used; greeks returns a plist of sensitivities. An engine that cannot price an instrument signals unsupported-combination, or model-domain-error in a parameter regime its method does not cover; automatic selection moves on to the next engine. unsupported-quanto is signalled for an instrument in a currency other than its underlying's, and invalid-argument for a malformed argument.

  • [class] FINCL:INSTRUMENT

    A contract. Carries terms only: no market data, no model. Instruments are immutable and reusable across scenarios.

  • [class] FINCL:ENGINE

  • [function] FINCL:MAKE-ENGINE KEY &REST SETTINGS

    The engine designated by KEY (:black-scholes, :baw, :rgw, :binomial, :cos, :fd, :mc, :merton-series, :cev, or any class's (:designator ...)) with SETTINGS, its initargs, evaluated. :PROCESS goes to engines that take one; an engine whose model is fixed (Black-Scholes, BAW, RGW) accepts a process only of its model, with no parameters set (it prices with the market's data), and takes the process's DIVIDEND-MODEL setting if it has one of its own.

    (make-engine :binomial :n-steps 4000) (make-engine :cos :process (make-heston :xi 0.5d0) :n-terms 512)

  • [generic-function] FINCL:PRICE INSTRUMENT ENGINE MARKET

    Returns (values npv standard-error engine). The second value is NIL for deterministic engines; the third is the engine that priced. ENGINE may be an engine designator (src/engines/selection.lisp): a keyword such as :binomial, a list (:cos :n-terms 512) as data, a process to price under with an automatically chosen engine, or :auto for GBM.

    (price opt (make-engine :binomial :n-steps 4000) m) (price opt (make-heston :xi 0.5d0) m)

  • [generic-function] FINCL:GREEKS INSTRUMENT ENGINE MARKET &KEY WHICH BUMPS METHOD BATCHES

    Sensitivities of INSTRUMENT's price under ENGINE in MARKET, as a plist with the keys in WHICH (default every key of GREEK-KEYS: :delta :gamma :vega :theta :rho :dividend-rho :vanna :volga). BUMPS overrides DEFAULT-GREEK-BUMPS for the methods that bump. Monte Carlo results also carry :standard-errors, a plist with the same keys.

    (greeks call (make-instance 'black-scholes-engine) m :which '(:delta :vega)) => (:DELTA 0.6368... :VEGA 37.52...)

  • [condition] FINCL:UNSUPPORTED-COMBINATION FINCL:PRICING-ERROR

  • [condition] FINCL:MODEL-DOMAIN-ERROR FINCL:PRICING-ERROR

    The engine has a method for the instrument, but its approximation does not hold in this market regime.

  • [condition] FINCL:UNSUPPORTED-QUANTO FINCL:PRICING-ERROR

    An instrument paying in a currency other than its underlying's: pricing it needs an FX vol and correlation fincl does not model.

  • [condition] FINCL:INVALID-ARGUMENT FINCL:INVALID-INPUT TYPE-ERROR

    An argument of the wrong type or shape. Also a TYPE-ERROR, so handlers written for CHECK-TYPE keep working.

4.2 Black-Scholes

Closed forms in the forward, the discount factor and the total variance to expiry, so they hold under term structures of rates, dividends and vol. European vanillas; continuous barriers (Reiner-Rubinstein) and discrete monitoring through the continuity correction of Broadie, Glasserman and Kou; discrete geometric Asians.

See F. Black and M. Scholes, The pricing of options and corporate liabilities, Journal of Political Economy 81 (1973); E. Reiner and M. Rubinstein, Breaking down the barriers, Risk 4 (1991); M. Broadie, P. Glasserman and S. Kou, A continuity correction for discrete barrier options, Mathematical Finance 7 (1997); A. Kemna and A. Vorst, A pricing method for options based on average asset values, Journal of Banking and Finance 14 (1990).

  • [class] FINCL:BLACK-SCHOLES-ENGINE FINCL:ANALYTIC-ENGINE

    Black's formula for European vanillas.

4.3 Barone-Adesi-Whaley

Quadratic approximation of the American premium over the European price. Returns the European price where early exercise is never optimal.

See G. Barone-Adesi and R. E. Whaley, Efficient analytic approximation of American option values, Journal of Finance 42 (1987).

  • [class] FINCL:BARONE-ADESI-WHALEY-ENGINE FINCL:ANALYTIC-ENGINE

    Quadratic approximation for American vanillas.

4.4 Merton series

European vanillas under Merton jump diffusion as a Poisson-weighted sum of Black-Scholes prices conditional on the number of jumps.

See R. C. Merton, Option pricing when underlying stock returns are discontinuous, Journal of Financial Economics 3 (1976).

  • [class] FINCL:MERTON-SERIES-ENGINE FINCL:ANALYTIC-ENGINE

    Merton's (1976) series for European vanillas under a jump diffusion: Black prices conditional on the number of jumps, weighted by their Poisson probabilities.

    (make-instance 'merton-series-engine :process (make-instance 'merton-jump :intensity 1))

← ↑ → ↺

4.5 CEV formula

European vanillas under CEV with $\beta\le 1$ and absorption at zero, in terms of the noncentral $\chi^2$ distribution.

See R. Schroder, Computing the constant elasticity of variance option pricing formula, Journal of Finance 44 (1989).

  • [class] FINCL:CEV-ENGINE FINCL:ANALYTIC-ENGINE

    Schroder's (1989) noncentral chi-square formula for European vanillas under CEV with absorption at zero, beta <= 1.

    (make-instance 'cev-engine :process (make-instance 'cev :beta 0.5d0))

← ↑ → ↺

4.6 Binomial tree

Cox-Ross-Rubinstein tree for GBM. By default the last step is valued with the Black-Scholes formula to remove the odd-even oscillation (BBS), and the price is $2v(N)-v(N/2)$ (BBSR). Bermudan dates snap to the nearest step.

  • n-steps: steps in the tree

  • smooth: Black-Scholes on the last step

  • extrapolate: Richardson extrapolation

See J. C. Cox, S. A. Ross and M. Rubinstein, Option pricing: a simplified approach, Journal of Financial Economics 7 (1979); M. Broadie and J. Detemple, American option valuation: new bounds, approximations, and a comparison of existing methods, Review of Financial Studies 9 (1996).

  • [class] FINCL:BINOMIAL-ENGINE FINCL:ENGINE

    Cox-Ross-Rubinstein binomial tree.

    (make-instance 'binomial-engine :n-steps 5000)

← ↑ → ↺

4.7 COS

Fourier-cosine expansion of the density of the log-return, with coefficients from the characteristic function. Europeans need only the characteristic function; Bermudans and Americans use the backward recursion, which needs state-independent log-increments, with the American price extrapolated from Bermudans of $M, 2M, 4M, 8M$ dates.

  • n-terms: terms in the expansion

  • truncation: half-width $L$ of the truncation range, in cumulant scale

  • richardson-dates: $M$

See F. Fang and C. W. Oosterlee, A novel pricing method for European options based on Fourier-cosine series expansions, SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 31 (2008), and Pricing early-exercise and discrete barrier options by Fourier-cosine series expansions, Numerische Mathematik 114 (2009).

  • [class] FINCL:COS-ENGINE FINCL:ENGINE

    Fang and Oosterlee's Fourier-cosine (COS) method. Europeans need only a characteristic function; Bermudans and Americans use the backward recursion of their 2009 paper, which needs a process whose log-increments are independent of the state (the INDEPENDENT-INCREMENTS mixin; GBM).

    (make-instance 'cos-engine :process (make-instance 'heston) :n-terms 160)

← ↑ → ↺

4.8 Finite differences

Crank-Nicolson on a log-spot grid concentrated at the strike, with Rannacher start-up after each payoff discontinuity. Americans and Bermudans by Ikonen-Toivanen; barriers by truncating the grid or by events on the monitoring dates. Heston by Hundsdorfer-Verwer ADI on a (log-spot, variance) grid. Jump processes are refused.

  • n-space, n-variance, n-time: grid sizes

  • theta: time-stepping weight, $\frac{1}{2}$ is Crank-Nicolson

  • rannacher-steps: implicit substeps after a discontinuity

  • concentration, width: grid concentration and half-width in standard deviations

See R. Rannacher, Finite element solution of diffusion problems with irregular data, Numerische Mathematik 43 (1984); S. Ikonen and J. Toivanen, Operator splitting methods for American option pricing, Applied Mathematics Letters 17 (2004); K. J. in 't Hout and S. Foulon, ADI finite difference schemes for option pricing in the Heston model with correlation, International Journal of Numerical Analysis and Modeling 7 (2010).

  • [class] FINCL:FD-ENGINE FINCL:ENGINE

    Finite differences in log-spot: a theta-scheme with Rannacher start-up on a sinh-concentrated grid, Ikonen-Toivanen splitting for American exercise, continuous barriers as boundary conditions; Heston by Hundsdorfer-Verwer ADI.

    (make-engine :fd :process (make-local-vol) :n-space 400 :n-time 400)

← ↑ → ↺

4.9 Monte Carlo

Simulates the paths of any process that defines a stepper. Terminal-only payoffs sample the terminal value exactly where the process allows; the rest step through a time grid containing every observation date. Americans and Bermudans by least-squares regression on the continuation value. Barriers monitored continuously are corrected with the Brownian bridge. Prices do not depend on the number of threads or chunks.

  • process, n-paths, n-steps, seed

  • rng: :counter (counter-based Philox), :sobol (randomized quasi-Monte Carlo) or a random-state generator

  • antithetic: antithetic pairs

  • control-variates: :european, :geometric-asian

See P. Glasserman, Monte Carlo Methods in Financial Engineering (2004); F. A. Longstaff and E. S. Schwartz, Valuing American options by simulation: a simple least-squares approach, Review of Financial Studies 14 (2001); J. K. Salmon, M. A. Moraes, R. O. Dror and D. E. Shaw, Parallel random numbers: as easy as 1, 2, 3, Proceedings of SC11 (2011); S. Joe and F. Y. Kuo, Constructing Sobol sequences with better two-dimensional projections, SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 30 (2008).

  • [class] FINCL:MONTE-CARLO-ENGINE FINCL:ENGINE

5 Simulation

simulate is a construct that allows one to simulate paths of a process or processes, and evaluates lisp forms on a list of days in the simulation. Paths come from the process protocol that the Monte Carlo engine uses. The lisp forms evaluated and tracked on these days can be :state variables from the process, or symbols declared in :start and :hold. This allows trading and hedging strategies to be simulated, where one can write trackers for PnL, max drawdown, etc. Because the maximum resolution of the simulation is daily actions, these simulations cannot model complex intraday statistics and strategies. 

(simulate (process market dates &key n-paths seed rng antithetic n-steps max-dt
                                     correlation keep market-from-state)
  (:accumulate (var init) ...)          ; per-path doubles
  (:hold (var init) ...)                ; per-path objects
  (:portfolio var [init] &key record engine cash cash-curve collect)  ; a per-path book, one clause each
  (:state var name [underlying])        ; a state component, e.g. :variance
  (:start [([market] &key time)] form ...)          ; at the valuation date
  (:every (date [market] &key time dt) form ...)    ; at each of DATES
  (:on date-list (i [market] &key time dt) form ...) ; at the dates of a list
  (:final (name form) ...))             ; named per-path outputs

Within :start, :every and :on bodies, (record name value) adds the real VALUE to the statistic NAME (a keyword) at the current date, and returns VALUE; (stop) ends the path, after which its later dates run no clauses and record nothing (:final still runs); (previous var) is a :state variable's value at the previous date. All are matched by name in any package. time and dt bind the date's market time and its step from the previous date. MARKET is the date's market within the simulation, holding its curves, surfaces and dividends; it is built lazily, only doing so if the forms use it.

Recording. A statistic is over everything recorded under NAME at a date, so it summarizes the paths at that date. Recording at the same date more than once under one name pools the values, and a date where nothing was recorded holds NaN (a count of 0). record in :start goes to the valuation date. Recorded values are streamed into summarizing statistics, each (name, date) keeps the count, mean, standard deviation, minimum and maximum, and :keep :quantiles adds quantiles. Read the results with (simulation-statistic result name statistic), which gives a vector over simulation-dates, and list the names with simulation-recorded-names. Use record for a quantity followed through time, such as a hedging balance or a spot, and a :final output for the per-path quantity at the end such as profit-loss, since :final keeps one value per path. In a :portfolio clause the key :record lists :value and Greek keys to record each date under the names (var-keyword key), e.g. (:book :delta) for the book book and the key :delta. Several :portfolio clauses run several books on the same paths, so their outputs are paired; without :final each book's value is an output named by its variable.

Outputs. Each :final entry is a number per path for each path. All outputs come from the same paths, so a quantity computed from several (a difference, a ratio, an indicator) is a paired estimate on common random numbers: simulation-summary gives its mean and standard error, with antithetic pairs averaged first, and the standard error of a difference $X-Y$ is $\sqrt{(\sigma_X^2+\sigma_Y^2-2\rho\sigma_X\sigma_Y)/n}$, small when the outputs are correlated. For example, the following code simulates a delta-hedging strategy against a short call position, comparing :hedged strategy against an :unhedged strategy. At each date, the underlying is traded to neutralize the net delta exposure of the holdings.

(simulate ((make-gbm) *market* *dates* :n-paths 20000)
  (:accumulate (shares 0d0) (cash 0d0) (premium 0d0))
  (:state s :spot)
  (:start (market)
    ;; Sell the call option to open the position
    (setf premium (price call :black-scholes market))
    (incf cash premium))
  (:every (date market)
    (when (date< date expiry)
      (let ((delta (getf (greeks call :black-scholes market :which '(:delta)) :delta)))
        ;; Neutralize delta exposure
        (decf cash (* (- delta shares) s))
        (setf shares delta))))
  ;; Return final balances
  (:final (:hedged (+ cash (* shares s) (- (payoff-value (option-payoff call) s))))
          (:unhedged (- premium (payoff-value (option-payoff call) s)))))

Collection. record keeps count, mean, variance, min and max per name and date, merged in chunk order; :keep :quantiles keeps the values for quantiles, :keep (:paths k) the first $k$ paths' states, held objects (whose markets path-markets rebuilds) and recorded values (simulation-path-record), :keep :states every path's.

Several underlyings. PROCESS may be a list of (underlying process) pairs, each stepped on its own view. :correlation ((u1 u2 rho) ...) correlates the Brownian motions $W^{(1)}, W^{(2)}$ driving the two spots: with spot-loadings $a, b$ ($W^{(1)}=\sum_k a_k Z^{(1)}_k$, unit norm), factors $i$ and $j$ are correlated at $\rho\,a_i b_j$, so $\operatorname{corr}(W^{(1)}, W^{(2)})=\rho$ whatever the processes' own factors (Heston's variance shock included). A square matrix over every factor gives full control.

(simulate (((:aapl (make-heston)) (:msft (make-gbm))) market dates
           :correlation '((:aapl :msft 0.6d0)) :keep (:paths 20))
  (:state a :spot :aapl)
  (:state m :spot :msft)
  (:every (date) (record :basket (+ (* 2 a) m))))

Clauses must not start parallel work: paths already run in parallel, and pricing inside a clause is serial.

See P. Glasserman, Monte Carlo Methods in Financial Engineering (2004); D. Bertsimas, L. Kogan and A. W. Lo, When is time continuous?, Journal of Financial Economics 55 (2000).

5.1 Simulated market components

By default only the spots move along a path: on each date the market is rebuilt with the simulated spots, and its curves, vol surfaces, dividends and fixings stay as they were on day 0. A simulation can also rebuild those other parts on each date, either from a process or from a formula.

Two words are used below. The world is what determines the market on each date: the processes stepped along the paths and the :derive forms. The desk is the clause code: what is held, how it is priced, when it is hedged, and which model it prices with. The desk reads the world's markets; nothing the desk does changes the paths.

What a run measures. Every process here is risk-neutral: the average discounted price of anything the world prices stays equal to today's price. A strategy whose prices agree with the world therefore has an average discounted P&L of 0. A run measures the spread of the P&L (hedging error), the cost of a decision rule (when to exercise, when to call), exposure and its tails, and what the desk gains or loses when its prices differ from the world's. For example, a 2-year at-the-money straddle marked by Black-Scholes on a 20% surface, in a Heston world with vol of vol 0.5, is worth 22.51 to the desk and 19.75 in the world, and its average mark falls from the first to the second over its life.

Targets. A component is named in the factor syntax of bump: (:spot u), (:vol u), (:dividend u), (:dividend-schedule u) and (:curve id); a bare keyword u means (:spot u). market-component reads one from a market.

Legs. PROCESS may be a list of legs (target process &rest options), each stepped on its own; the options go to structure-at-state, which turns the leg's state on a date into its component. process-targets lists the kinds a process can drive. :correlation and (:state var name leg) name a leg by its target or, for a spot, its underlying.

(simulate ((((:spot :spx) (make-heston))
            ((:curve :usd-ois) (make-hull-white :a 0.03d0 :sigma 0.008d0) :knots tenors))
           market dates :correlation (((:spot :spx) (:curve :usd-ois) 0.2d0)))
  ...)

Formulas. (:derive target ([market [date]]) form ...) sets a component on every date from the date, the market built so far and the :state variables. Its forms see nothing else of the path (no :accumulate or :hold variable, no record), so a component depends only on the date and the simulated states, and path-markets rebuilds it exactly.

(:derive (:curve :usd-libor-3m) (market)      ; a basis over a simulated curve
  (bump-structure (market-component market '(:curve :usd-ois)) '(:absolute 0.0015d0)))
(:derive (:vol :spx) (market date)            ; a surface per date from a table
  (gethash date surfaces))

Hull-White curves. A Hull-White leg simulates the short rate $r = x + \alpha(t)$, $\alpha(t) = f(0,t) + \frac{\sigma^2}{2a^2}(1-e^{-at})^2$, and its integral exactly: over a step $\Delta$, $x_{t+\Delta} = x_t e^{-a\Delta} + \varepsilon_1$ and $\int_t^{t+\Delta} x\,ds = x_t B(\Delta) + \varepsilon_2$, $B(\Delta) = (1-e^{-a\Delta})/a$, with $(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2)$ Gaussian, $\operatorname{Var}\varepsilon_1 = \frac{\sigma^2}{2a}(1-e^{-2a\Delta})$, $\operatorname{Var}\varepsilon_2 = \frac{\sigma^2}{a^2}\big(\Delta - 2B(\Delta) + \frac{1-e^{-2a\Delta}}{2a}\big)$, $\operatorname{Cov} = \frac{\sigma^2}{2}B(\Delta)^2$. The curve on a date is the model's own bond price function $\tau \mapsto P(t,t+\tau\mid r_t)$ (state-discount-bond), exact at every maturity and evaluated only when a price asks for it; with :knots (tenors) it is those bond prices log-linear in discount factors between the knots, as before task 33, and only that curve has a snapshot-form. Every rates instrument prices on either unchanged. market-curve-discount reads a curve of a market with several underlyings. (path-discount [curve]) in a clause is $\exp(-\int_0^t r\,ds)$ on the path when a leg simulates that curve, and today's discount factor otherwise, so its average is today's discount factor. :fix (list index ...) records each index's fixing on each date from that date's simulated curve, and later markets on the path carry them; pricing needs every fixing date among DATES (fixing-dates).

Derived surfaces. rescale-vol-structure keeps a surface's smile and sets its at-the-money level to a function $a(\tau)$: $w'(k,\tau) = w(k,\tau)\,a(\tau)/w(0,\tau)$ in total variance at log-moneyness $k$. Driven by a simulated Heston variance $v$, $a(\tau) = \theta\tau + (v-\theta)\frac{1-e^{-\kappa\tau}}{\kappa}$, the expected variance to $\tau$, so a shock to $v$ fades over longer maturities as the model says. This level is the variance-swap level, a little above Heston's own implied variance when the vol of vol is not 0; with vol of vol 0 the two agree.

(simulate (heston market dates)
  (:state v :variance)
  (:derive (:vol :spx) (market)
    (rescale-vol-structure (market-component market '(:vol :spx))
                           (lambda (tau) (+ (* theta tau) (* (- v theta) (/ (- 1 (exp (- (* kappa tau)))) kappa))))))
  (:every (date market)
    (record :straddle (+ (price call :black-scholes market) (price put :black-scholes market)))))

The desk's model. The library never refits a model. A desk that refits keeps its model in :hold and calls calibrate on the date's market, for example to (surface-quotes market :spx options), the options quoted at the market's vols. Calibration inside a clause is serial, so results do not depend on the worker count. One warm-started Heston refit to six quotes with COS takes about 0.3 s, so a weekly refit over a year at 1,000 paths takes about four hours on one worker.

(:hold (desk heston-0))
(:every (date market)
  (setf desk (handler-bind ((convergence-failure #'use-best))
               (calibrate desk market (surface-quotes market :spx options) :engine :cos)))
  (record :mark (price straddle (make-engine :cos :process desk) market)))

Writing a component model. A process drives a non-spot component when it lists the kind in process-targets, steps with make-stepper and defines structure-at-state; a curve process that names a state component :integral (the integral of its short rate) is discounted by path-discount. A :derive form can also move a component by an independent factor, such as a random level scaling every implied vol by $e^x$. Then the average surface rises (with $\operatorname{Var} x = 0.04$ after a year, every vol averages 2% above today's), the stock's own volatility does not follow the surface, a shock moves all maturities alike, and a parametric surface (SSVI) changes shape as well as level. The library ships only components computed from the world's own state.

Pitfalls.

  • Rates move together. Hull-White has one source of randomness, so every maturity of the curve moves in the same direction: over a month the 2-year and 10-year zero rates move with correlation 1.00. A trade that profits when the curve steepens shows almost no P&L, and hedging a 7-year bond with 2- and 10-year ones, rebalanced 24 times over 19 months, leaves a residual of about 0.01% of its value. Neither says anything about the real trade.

  • The stock does not feel the rates. The stock is stepped on today's rates, not on the rates simulated beside it. Uncorrelated with them, its average discounted price stays at today's spot; correlated, it does not: at correlation 0.5 with a Hull-White rate ($\sigma = 1\%$) the average discounted price of a 100 stock after 5 years is 98.8.

  • A derived surface keeps today's shape. rescale-vol-structure moves the level of the surface and keeps today's smile: if the 1-year 90-strike vol is 1.7 points above the at-the-money 20%, it is 2.5 points above an at-the-money 30%. A strategy that depends on the skew changing is not captured.

  • A refit changes the desk, not the world. Refitting a model in a clause changes the prices and hedges the clause sees. The stock's paths are the same, bit for bit, with or without the refit.

Glossary.

  • World: the legs and :derive forms, which determine the market on each date.

  • Target: the name of one market component, such as (:curve :usd-ois).

  • Derived component: a component set on each date by a :derive form.

See J. Hull and A. White, Pricing interest-rate-derivative securities, The Review of Financial Studies 3 (1990); P. Glasserman, Monte Carlo Methods in Financial Engineering (2004), section 3.3; R. Ahmad and P. Wilmott, Which free lunch would you like today, sir?, Wilmott Magazine (2005).

5.2 Books with cash

A :portfolio given :cash x (or holding a cash-account position in its INIT) is self-financing: cash is a position of x units of a cash-account, priced at 1 under any engine, so the book's value, Greeks, snapshots and P&L explain include it. Negative cash is borrowing at the same rate; there is no credit limit, transaction cost or margin.

On each date $t_k$, $k \ge 1$, of a path still running, in order:

  1. Interest. The balance is multiplied by $G_k = D{k-1}/D_k$, $D$ the path's discount on the curve of the cash (:cash-curve id, default the currency's discount curve): $e^{\int{t{k-1}}^{t_k} r\,ds}$ when a leg simulates that curve, so the discounted balance is constant between trades, and $P(0,t{k-1})/P(0,t_k)$ on the initial curve otherwise.

  2. Income. Each position pays its quantity times its income over $(t_{k-1}, t_k]$ into cash, for the kinds in :collect (default :all):

  3. a holding, its discrete dividends going ex in the interval, on the ex-date: a cash dividend $D$ its price drop $D\,P(t{pay})/P(t{ex})$, so the drop and the cash cancel and no receivable is carried; a proportional one $\delta$ times the spot expected just before the drop (:discrete-dividend); and its continuous yield to first order, $S_{k-1}(e^{q\Delta t}-1)$ (:continuous-dividend);

  4. a bond, its coupons (:coupon) and redemption (:redemption); a swap or FRA, its signed flows (:coupon), on their payment dates;

  5. an option, its payoff at expiry (:expiry), which must be one of the dates (early exercise is clause code: trade it to 0, deposit the intrinsic value);

  6. cash, caps, floors and swaptions nothing.

A flow paid at $t_p < t_k$ is grown to $t_k$ by $P(0,t_p)/P(0,t_k)$ without a leg on the cash's curve, exactly, and credited at face with one: an error of about the amount times $r(t_k - t_p)$, at most 0.33% of the flow for monthly dates at 4%. Then the bonds, swaps and FRAs whose last flow is paid and the options that expired leave the book. 3. The :every and :on clauses, in order. (trade book instrument &key by to engine) buys or sells in BOOK at the date's price (ENGINE, else the position's engine, else the book's :engine, with price-portfolio's fallbacks), paid from its cash, and returns the cash paid; a position sold to 0 leaves the book, and a price that fails signals. (deposit book x) and (withdraw book x) move cash in and out. adjust-position stays a free change of quantity. 4. The book's :record keys, :value, :cash and Greeks.

At :start only steps 3 and 4 run; after stop nothing accrues.

A negative dividend yield as a drift. A user giving an equity $q < 0$ to make it grow faster than the rate is, in a book, debited $|q| S\,\Delta t$ a period as a continuous dividend: :collect '(:discrete-dividend :coupon :redemption :expiry) leaves it out, and the yield then acts only on the spot's drift.

A retirement drawdown, 60% in an equity and 40% in a 6-year par bond rolled monthly, 5% a year withdrawn, the bond on the exact Hull-White curve:

(simulate ((((:curve :usd) (make-hull-white :a 0.05d0 :sigma 0.01d0)) (:eq (make-gbm)))
           market dates :n-paths 20000 :keep (:paths 10)
           :correlation ((:eq (:curve :usd) 0.2d0)))
  (:portfolio book (make-portfolio "60/40") :cash 1 :engine :discounting
              :collect '(:discrete-dividend :coupon :redemption) :record (:value))
  (:hold (bond nil))
  (:every (date market)
    (withdraw book (/ 0.05d0 12))
    (when bond (trade book bond :to 0))               ; sell last month's bond
    (let ((v (price book :discounting market)))       ; cash and the equity
      (if (<= v 0)
          (stop)
          (progn
            (setf bond (par-bond date 6 (par-rate market 6)))   ; user code
            (trade book (make-holding :underlying :eq) :to (/ (* 0.6d0 v) (market-component market '(:spot :eq))))
            (trade book bond :to (/ (* 0.4d0 v) (price bond :discounting market)))))))
  (:final (:ruined (if (<= (price book :discounting market) 0) 1d0 0d0))))

A book with bonds prices with :discounting: holdings and cash ignore the engine, and :auto is refused for rates instruments. (paths result :record '(:book :value)) in fincl-viz draws the book's value on the kept paths.

6 Stochastic processes

A process is a stochastic differential equation. For the purposes of financial modeling, these are typically drift-diffusion processes or Lévy processes. However, fincl allows you to define your own (see TODO).

A general drift-diffusion process has the form $$dS_t = \mu(X_t, t) + \sigma(X_t,t)dW_t,$$ where $\mu(X_t,t)$ is called the drift, and $\sigma(X_t,t)$ is the diffusion. $W_t$ is a standard Brownian motion.

The Lévy processes add a jump term to drift-diffusion models dictated by a Poisson process. All processes forms are given under the risk-netural measure, where $r$ is the risk-free rate, $q$ is an equity dividend.

6.2 Lévy processes

Lévy processes add jump terms to drift-diffusion models, accounting for random jumps seen in the price movement of various instruments. Throughout, $dN_t$ will denote a Poisson process with rate $\lambda$.

← ↑ → ↺

6.2.1 Merton Jump Diffusion

$$\frac{dS_t}{S_t} = (r-q-\lambda k)\,dt + \sigma\,dW_t + (e^{Y}-1)\,dN_t, \qquad Y \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_J, \sigma_J^2),$$

with $k = e^{\mu_J + \sigma_J^2/2} - 1$.

  • $\sigma$: volatility of the diffusion

  • $\lambda$: jump intensity

  • $\mu_J$, $\sigma_J$: mean and standard deviation of the log jump $Y$

See R. C. Merton, Option pricing when underlying stock returns are discontinuous, Journal of Financial Economics 3 (1976).

6.2.3 Variance Gamma

$$S_t = S_0\exp\left((r-q+\omega)\,t + \theta \, G_t + \sigma \, W_{G_t}\right), \qquad \omega = \frac1\nu\ln\left(1 - \theta\nu - \tfrac12\sigma^2\nu\right),$$

where $G$ is a gamma process with $\mathbb{E}[G_t] = t$ and $\operatorname{Var}[G_t] = \nu t$.

  • $\theta$: drift of the Brownian motion, setting the skewness

  • $\sigma$: volatility of the Brownian motion

  • $\nu$: variance rate of the gamma clock, setting the kurtosis

See D. B. Madan, P. P. Carr and E. C. Chang, The variance gamma process and option pricing, European Finance Review 2 (1998).

where $V$ is an inverse Gaussian process with $\mathbb{E}[V_t] = \delta t/\gamma$ and $\gamma = \sqrt{\alpha^2-\beta^2}$.

6.3 Change of measure

Every process is stated under the risk-neutral measure $Q$. A process under the physical measure $P$, for scenarios rather than prices, comes from one by Girsanov's theorem: a state $X^i$ driven by $W^i$ with diffusion coefficient $\sigma_i$ has

$$dW^{i,Q}_t = dW^{i,P}_t + \lambda_i(t, X_t)\,dt, \qquad \mu^P_i = \mu^Q_i + \sigma_i\lambda_i,$$

$\lambda_i$ the market price of risk of $W^i$. Diffusion coefficients and correlations are the same under both measures; jump parts change through their intensity and size law.

Premia are named by state (:spot, :variance, :short-rate), given as

  • a price of risk $\lambda$: a real, (:scaled c) for $\lambda = c\,\sigma$ (a drift change $c\,\sigma^2$, proportional to variance), or a function of time and state;

  • a drift change $\delta = \sigma\lambda$, in the state's coordinate (for the spot, the excess growth rate: $E^P[S_{t+h}] = E^Q[S_{t+h}]\,e^{\delta h}$ for constant $\delta$);

  • physical values of measure-dependent parameters.

| Process | Premium | Under $P$ | |---|---|---| | GBM, CEV, local vol | spot: any form | drift added after each step; exact when $\delta$ is constant over a step, trapezoid rule otherwise | | Merton, Kou | spot; jump parameters | as GBM; the jump law compensated at its $P$ intensity | | Heston, Bates | spot: any form; variance: (:scaled c) or a constant $d$; $\kappa$, $\theta$ | $\kappa^P = \kappa - c\,\xi^2$, $\theta^P = (\kappa\theta + d)/\kappa^P$ | | NGARCH | spot: real $\lambda$ per period | $\gamma^P = \gamma - \lambda$, mean $\lambda\sqrt{h_t}$ | | Hull-White | short rate: any form, of time only; $a$ | drift $\sigma\lambda(t)$ inside the exact step | | variance gamma, NIG | $\sigma, \theta$; $\alpha, \beta$ | physical Lévy measure, drift beside the jumps unchanged |

The measures stay equivalent: states without a Brownian driver take no price of risk, and variance gamma's $\nu$ and NIG's $\delta$, which set the small jumps, are fixed (K. Sato, Lévy Processes and Infinitely Divisible Distributions, Theorem 33.1). A Heston variance premium keeps the measures equivalent only if the Feller condition $2\kappa\theta > \xi^2$ holds under both (P. Cheridito, D. Filipović and R. L. Kimmel, Market price of risk specifications for affine models, Journal of Financial Economics 83 (2007)); a violation signals feller-condition-violated. The variance premium is Heston's, proportional to variance (S. L. Heston, Review of Financial Studies 6 (1993)); the NGARCH form is Duan's (_Mathematical Finance 5_ (1995)).

In SIMULATE a physical process is a leg's process: its state evolves under $P$, and each date's market is built from that state with the risk-neutral process, so prices on every date are $Q$ valuations of a $P$ scenario. Engines and calibration refuse a physical process.

  • [function] FINCL:CHANGE-MEASURE PROCESS &KEY PRICES-OF-RISK DRIFT-CHANGES PARAMETERS

    A PHYSICAL-PROCESS stepping PROCESS's dynamics under the physical measure. PROCESS is a risk-neutral process with a stepper. Premia name the state they apply to, from PROCESS-STATE-NAMES; states not named carry none.

    PRICES-OF-RISK ((state spec) ...), lambda for the Brownian motion driving the state: a real (constant lambda); (:scaled c), lambda = c sigma, a drift change of c sigma^2 (proportional to variance); or a function (lambda (time state)) returning lambda, TIME in market time, STATE the state vector (read only). DRIFT-CHANGES ((state spec) ...), the drift change delta = sigma lambda itself, in the state's coordinate: a real or a function (lambda (time state)). For the observable, delta is the excess continuously compounded growth over the risk-neutral carry: E^P[S(t+h)] = E^Q[S(t+h)] exp(delta h) for constant delta. PARAMETERS a plist of physical values of PROCESS's measure-dependent parameters (jump intensities and size laws).

    Latent states take only premia that keep their scheme exact, as physical parameters of the stepping process: Heston's :VARIANCE (:SCALED c) gives kappa^P = kappa - c xi^2 and theta^P = kappa theta / kappa^P, a constant drift change d gives theta^P = (kappa theta + d) / kappa; NGARCH's :SPOT takes a real lambda per period (Duan's model, gamma^P = gamma - lambda); Hull-White's :SHORT-RATE takes any form, functions being called with STATE NIL (a function of time only), as a drift sigma lambda(t) inside its exact step. Heston variance premia keep the measures equivalent only when the Feller condition 2 kappa theta > xi^2 holds under both; a violation signals the warning FELLER-CONDITION-VIOLATED, once per image. Variance gamma and NIG take physical parameters only, plus the drift that keeps their drift beside the jumps unchanged.

    A drift change of the observable is added after each step of the risk-neutral scheme, exactly when delta is constant over a step, by the trapezoid rule on the states at the step's two ends otherwise. Discrete dividends are applied once, after it. With no premia the result steps as PROCESS on the same normals. Defaults left to the market (NIL parameters) are resolved, and the premia converted, by RESOLVE-PROCESS. Bad arguments signal INVALID-MEASURE-CHANGE.

    (change-measure (make-gbm) :drift-changes '((:spot 0.03d0))) (change-measure (make-merton-jump) :prices-of-risk '((:spot 0.2d0)) :parameters '(:intensity 1d0))

7 Portfolios and risk

A portfolio is a tree of positions, each a signed quantity $q_i$ of an instrument, valued on one market $M$ as $$V(M)=\sum_i q_i\,V_i(M).$$ Pricing is price on every position, in parallel and under a recovery policy: a book is priced even when one of its positions cannot be. Risk is revaluation on scenarios, which are other markets (bump, aging), so every instrument and engine that prices on a market takes part with no further method. Results do not depend on the number of workers.

(defparameter *market*
  (make-market :valuation-date #D"2026-10-01"
               :underlyings '((:spx :spot 5000 :currency :usd
                               :dividend 0.015d0 :vol 0.18d0))
               :curves '((:usd-ois :rate 0.04d0))))
(defparameter *book*
  (make-portfolio "book"
    (make-position (make-option (call 5000) (expires #T"1Y" :from #D"2026-10-01"))
                   :quantity 10 :tags '(:desk :a))
    (make-position (make-option (put 4500) (american) (expires #T"6M" :from #D"2026-10-01"))
                   :quantity -5 :tags '(:desk :b))
    (make-position (make-holding) :quantity -3 :tags '(:desk :a))))
(price *book* :auto *market*)
;; ==>  -11158.493465283193d0
;;      NIL
;;      NIL

7.2 Pricing a portfolio

price-portfolio returns a portfolio-result with one position-result per position, in the order of portfolio-positions: the unit price, its standard error, the engine that priced it and the condition it recovered from, read by position-result-unit-price, position-result-standard-error, position-result-engine and position-result-condition. A position that cannot be priced is recorded with its condition and nothing is signalled; portfolio-value returns the value, its standard error (Monte Carlo positions assumed independent) and the unpriced positions.

When an engine declines a position (unsupported-combination or model-domain-error), the engines in applicable-engines are tried in turn. That function finds the engines whose pricing methods accept the instrument by inspecting the method tables through the metaobject protocol, restricted to those that model the process, and orders them by engine-preference: Black-Scholes 10, Roll-Geske-Whaley 11, other closed forms 12, COS 20, finite differences 30, the tree 40, Monte Carlo 90. An engine listed may still decline in a regime its method does not cover.

(price-portfolio *book* *market*)
;; ==>  #<PORTFOLIO-RESULT "book" -11158.4935>
(applicable-engines (make-option (put 4500) (american) (expires #D"2027-04-01"))
                    *market* :process (make-heston))

7.4 Value at risk

With losses $\ell = - \Pi $ over $n$ scenarios sorted $\ell_1 \geq \ell_2 \geq \ldots $ and $ k =\lceil n(1-c)\rceil$, the historical value at risk and expected shortfall at confidence $c$ are $$\mathrm{VaR}_c = \ell_k,\qquad \mathrm{E S}_c = \frac{1}{k}\sum_{m = 1}^{k}\ell_m,$$ the $k$-th largest loss and the mean of the $k$ largest, the sample forms of the loss quantile and of the tail expectation. The sample is the P&L of a revaluation on historical-scenarios or on any scenario set.

The delta-normal VaR is $z_c\sqrt{s^\top C s}$ with $z_c$ the normal quantile, $C$ the covariance of the factor changes over the horizon and $s_f=\partial V/\partial x_f$ the portfolio's sensitivity to each factor, by central differences on bumped markets (factor-sensitivities); it is exact for a portfolio linear in jointly normal factors. repair-positive-semidefinite raises the negative eigenvalues of a covariance or correlation estimate to zero, which gives the nearest positive-semidefinite matrix in the Frobenius norm.

;; RETURNS: 500 daily SPX returns
(let* ((scenarios (historical-scenarios *market* `(((:spot :spx) :relative ,returns))))
       (pnl (scenario-pnl (revalue *book* *market* scenarios))))
  (values (historical-var pnl 0.99d0) (expected-shortfall pnl 0.99d0)))
(delta-normal-var *book* *market* '(((:spot :spx) :relative))
                  #2A((0.0004d0)) 0.99d0)

See C. Acerbi and D. Tasche, On the coherence of expected shortfall, Journal of Banking and Finance 26 (2002); R. T. Rockafellar and S. Uryasev, Conditional value-at-risk for general loss distributions, Journal of Banking and Finance 26 (2002); J. P. Morgan and Reuters, RiskMetrics Technical Document, 4th ed. (1996); N. J. Higham, Computing a nearest symmetric positive semidefinite matrix, Linear Algebra and its Applications 103 (1988).

7.5 Ladders and P&L explain

curve-delta-ladder gives the portfolio's sensitivity to each input quote of a bootstrapped curve, with the curve rebootstrapped on every bump; the sum is the parallel-shift sensitivity to first order. vega-ladder gives vega by expiry bucket.

explain-pnl attributes the P&L between two markets to the Greeks at the first, position by position: $$\Delta V\approx\delta\,\Delta S+\tfrac12\gamma\,\Delta S^2 +\nu\,\Delta\sigma+\text{vanna}\,\Delta S\,\Delta\sigma +\tfrac12\text{volga}\,\Delta\sigma^2+\theta\,\tau +\rho\,\Delta r+\rho_q\,\Delta q,$$ with $\Delta\sigma$ the move of the Black vol at the position's strike and expiry and $\tau$ the calendar days between the valuation dates. It returns each term, their sum :explained, the full revaluation :actual and the :unexplained remainder: third-order terms, rate convexity and cross terms with rates.

(explain-pnl *book* *market* (bump *market* '(:spot :spx) '(:relative 1/100)))
;; ==>  (:DELTA 181.67 :GAMMA 2.862 ... :EXPLAINED 184.53
;;       :ACTUAL 184.56 :UNEXPLAINED 0.0287)
(vega-ladder *book* *market* (list #T"3M" #T"1Y"))

8 Extending fincl

New currencies, calendars, processes and payoffs are defined with define- macros, which write classes, constructors and the methods the engines call. The dice game below uses all of them; each subsection defines one piece and the last assembles them into a toy option contract pricing.

A fair die is rolled once a week. Players are paid in casino chips, and two contracts are written on it:

  • Jackpot: pays the face of the last roll, plus a bonus if it shows the top face. With a bonus of 10 on a six-sided die the expected payoff is $3.5 + 10/6$.

8.3 define-process

Define a custom process, its constructor make-name and its parameters, which are validated, bounded and enumerable for calibration. The dynamics are ordinary methods:

  • initial-state: the state vector at time zero. Element 0 is the observable that payoffs see; the others are latent.

  • make-stepper: returns (lambda (state normals j)), which advances the state vector in place from times[j] to times[j+1] using standard normals, and returns NIL. Do expensive work outside the lambda.

  • process-state-names: names for the state elements, which path payoffs refer to.

The constructor takes the parameters as keywords. process-parameter reads one. Our process will model a fair die with faces faces. The state of the process will consist of 2 values: the value of the most recent roll, and a running total of all previous rolls.

(define-process fair-die (stochastic-process) (:state-size 2)
  ((faces 6d0 :bounds (2 nil) :doc "Number of faces."))
  (:documentation "A die rolled once per step. Element 0 is the latest roll,
element 1 the running total."))

(defmethod initial-state ((p fair-die) market)
  (declare (ignore market))
  (make-array 2 :element-type 'double-float :initial-element 0d0))

(defmethod process-state-names ((p fair-die)) '(:spot :total))

(defmethod make-stepper ((p fair-die) market times)
  (declare (ignore market times))
  (let ((faces (process-parameter p :faces)))
    (declare (type double-float faces))
    (lambda (state normals j)
      (declare (type (simple-array double-float (*)) state normals) (ignore j))
      ;; convert standard normal to discrete uniform U(1,FACES)
      (let ((roll (+ 1d0 (min (- faces 1d0)
                              (ffloor (* faces (norm-cdf (aref normals 0))))))))
        (setf (aref state 0) roll
              (aref state 1) (+ (aref state 1) roll)))
      nil)))

A parameter given as (name nil :market-default fn) is taken from the market when left unset; (name default :parameter nil) is a setting, not a model parameter.

  • [macro] FINCL:DEFINE-PROCESS NAME SUPERCLASSES (&KEY STATE-SIZE FACTORS) SLOTS &REST CLASS-OPTIONS

    Define the process class NAME and its constructor MAKE-NAME. SUPERCLASSES defaults to (STOCHASTIC-PROCESS). STATE-SIZE and FACTORS, when given, define PROCESS-STATE-SIZE and PROCESS-FACTORS (both default to 1). Each of SLOTS is

    (slot-name default &key bounds market-default (parameter t) measure-dependent doc reader type)

    • DEFAULT is the initform, evaluated per instance. NIL, with a MARKET-DEFAULT, means "take it from the market".

    • BOUNDS is (lo hi) in interval-designator form: a real is inclusive, (x) exclusive, NIL unbounded.

    • MARKET-DEFAULT names a function (process market horizon) -> double-float.

    • PARAMETER NIL declares a setting: validated, but not a model parameter (not in PROCESS-PARAMETERS or PARAMETER-VECTOR).

    • MEASURE-DEPENDENT T marks a parameter whose physical value may differ from its risk-neutral one (CHANGE-MEASURE's :PARAMETERS); the default NIL keeps it fixed under a change of measure.

    • READER defaults to SLOT-NAME, and the initarg is the keyword SLOT-NAME.

    • TYPE defaults to DOUBLE-FLOAT, or (OR(0 1) NULL(0 1) DOUBLE-FLOAT) when DEFAULT is NIL.

    MAKE-NAME takes the initargs of NAME and its DEFINE-PROCESS superclasses as keywords, passing only those given, so omitted ones keep their defaults. The class option (:constructor nil) leaves it out (abstract classes); it signals CONSTRUCTOR-COLLISION at load time if MAKE-NAME is already a function it did not define for NAME. Steppers, characteristic functions and samplers stay ordinary DEFMETHODs.

    (define-process heston (stochastic-process) (:state-size 2 :factors 2) ((v0 nil :market-default heston-default-v0 :bounds (0 nil) :doc "Initial variance.") (kappa 2d0 :bounds ((0) nil) :doc "Mean-reversion speed.")) (:documentation "Heston stochastic volatility."))

    (make-heston :kappa 1.5d0)

  • [class] FINCL:STOCHASTIC-PROCESS FINCL:PARAMETERIZED

    Dynamics of one or more state variables. Define concrete processes with DEFINE-PROCESS.

  • [class] FINCL:INDEPENDENT-INCREMENTS

    Mixin for processes whose log-increments over [t0, t1] do not depend on the state at t0 (Levy processes with deterministic time dependence, GBM among them). Such processes provide INCREMENT-CHARACTERISTIC-FUNCTION, and Fourier engines can then step backwards through exercise dates.

  • [function] FINCL:PROCESS-PARAMETERS PROCESS-OR-CLASS

    Names of the model parameters of a process, or a process class name, in declaration order. Settings declared with :PARAMETER NIL (a GARCH period) are excluded.

    (process-parameters 'heston) => (V0 KAPPA THETA XI RHO)

  • [function] FINCL:PROCESS-PARAMETER PROCESS NAME &OPTIONAL MARKET (HORIZON 1.0d0)

    The value of parameter or setting NAME of PROCESS. NAME is matched by symbol name, so a keyword works from any package. A NIL value means the parameter comes from the market. With MARKET, that default is resolved on MARKET at HORIZON (market time); without it, NIL is returned.

    (process-parameter (make-instance 'heston :rho -0.5d0) :rho) => -0.5d0 (process-parameter (make-instance 'heston) :v0) => NIL (process-parameter (make-instance 'heston) :v0 market) => 0.04d0

  • [function] FINCL:PARAMETER-VECTOR PROCESS MARKET &OPTIONAL (HORIZON 1.0d0)

    Independent standard normals consumed per time step.

  • [generic-function] FINCL:PROCESS-STATE-SIZE PROCESS

8.4 define-payoff

Define a payoff class from a formula of the observable at expiry. Slots are (name type &key initform doc fact); one without an initform is a required initarg. The class becomes a clause of make-option taking its slots as keywords. Calls and puts with a strike inherit from strike-payoff, which supplies phi and strike to the formula.

(define-payoff jackpot (payoff)
    ((top double-float :initform 6d0 :doc "The face that wins the bonus.")
     (bonus double-float :fact :bonus :doc "Paid on top of the face."))
  (s)
  (+ s (if (>= s top) bonus 0d0)))

Monte Carlo prices any such payoff. Closed-form engines only know the built-in ones, so :auto falls back to simulation for new payoffs.

  • [macro] FINCL:DEFINE-PAYOFF NAME SUPERCLASSES SLOTS (OBSERVABLE) &BODY BODY

    Define the payoff class NAME and derive PAYOFF-VALUE and PAYOFF-KERNEL from one formula. Each of SLOTS is (slot-name type &key initform reader doc fact); without INITFORM the initarg is required, and FACT names the construction fact that fills the slot (see MAKE-FROM-FACTS). BODY, optionally preceded by a docstring, computes the payoff from OBSERVABLE (a double-float) and may use every slot of NAME, and the variables its superclasses declare, as typed local variables. PAYOFF-KERNEL reads them once and closes over them, so the closure makes no generic call per evaluation.

    Inherit from data classes such as CALL-PUT-PAYOFF or STRIKE-PAYOFF, not from a payoff with its own formula: engines with a closed form for the parent would price the subclass with it. Doing so signals FORMULA-PAYOFF-SUPERCLASS at macroexpansion.

    (define-payoff digital (call-put-payoff) ((level double-float :doc "Pays 1 beyond this level.")) (s) (if (plusp (* phi (- s level))) 1d0 0d0))

  • [class] FINCL:CALL-PUT-PAYOFF FINCL:PAYOFF

    A payoff that comes as a call or a put.

  • [class] FINCL:STRIKE-PAYOFF FINCL:CALL-PUT-PAYOFF

    A call or put with a strike: the data a struck payoff needs, with no formula. Build new struck payoffs on this, not on VANILLA-PAYOFF: engines with closed forms for VANILLA-PAYOFF would apply them to a subclass and return the vanilla price.

    (define-payoff digital (strike-payoff) () (s) (if (plusp (* phi (- s strike))) 1d0 0d0))

    Dispatch once, return a typed (double-float -> double-float) closure. Engines call this before entering their loops.   • [condition] FINCL:FORMULA-PAYOFF-SUPERCLASS FINCL:INVALID-INPUT

    DEFINE-PAYOFF was given a payoff with its own formula as a superclass. Engines with closed forms for that superclass would apply them to the subclass and return the superclass's price.

8.5 define-path-payoff

Define a payoff that depends on the path, from clauses run on each path in the order written. In them s is the observable at the current grid point.

  • (:accumulate (var init)...): variables kept along a path

  • (:state var name): var is the process state element called name

  • (:start form...): at the valuation date

  • (:on slot (i) form...): on each date in the date-list slot

  • (:every form...): at every grid point

  • (:final form): the amount paid at expiry

(pay amount) inside :on pays on that date, and (stop) ends the path, which then pays nothing at expiry. The observation dates become points of the simulation grid. Path payoffs are European and priced by Monte Carlo.

The bust-game will refer to the fair-die state :total, which keeps the running total of die rolls. When the die is rolled on a date in rolls, check if the die rolls 1, the contract goes bust.

(define-path-payoff bust-game (payoff) ((rolls list))
  (s)
  (:state total :total)
  (:on rolls (i)
    (when (= s 1d0) (stop)))
  (:final total))

  • [macro] FINCL:DEFINE-PATH-PAYOFF NAME SUPERCLASSES SLOTS (OBSERVABLE) &BODY CLAUSES

    Define the payoff class NAME (a PATH-PAYOFF) and its PATH-KERNEL from CLAUSES. SLOTS are as in DEFINE-PAYOFF; a slot holding dates has type LIST. Every clause may use the slots and the variables SUPERCLASSES declare (PHI, STRIKE) as typed local variables, OBSERVABLE (the observable at the current grid point, a double-float), and the latent state variables of :STATE.

    (:accumulate (var init) ...) per-path double-float variables, set to INIT at the start of each path (:state var name) VAR is the state component NAME (STATE-INDEX), e.g. (:state v :variance) (:start form ...) at grid index 0 (the valuation date) (:on date-slot (index) form ...) at each date of DATE-SLOT; INDEX is the date's position in the slot (:every form ...) at every grid index (:final form) the amount paid at expiry

    In :ON clauses, (pay amount) pays AMOUNT on the current date and (pay amount i) on date I of the clause's slot. Clauses run in the order written; (stop) in any of them ends the path, which then pays nothing at expiry.

    (define-path-payoff autocallable (payoff) ((notional double-float) (initial double-float) (barrier double-float) (coupon double-float) (observations list)) (s) (:on observations (i) (when (>= s (* barrier initial)) (pay (+ notional coupon)) (stop))) (:final (min notional (* notional (/ s initial)))))

  • [class] FINCL:PATH-PAYOFF FINCL:PAYOFF

    A payoff defined by DEFINE-PATH-PAYOFF: its PATH-KERNEL holds all of its path logic, so it is European, combines only with PATH-INDEPENDENT, and has no closed form.

  • [generic-function] FINCL:PATH-KERNEL PAYOFF PATH-FEATURE PROCESS GRID

    (values init step final): typed closures over a per-path accumulator ACC, a (SIMPLE-ARRAY DOUBLE-FLOAT (*)) of PATH-ACCUMULATOR-SIZE.

    (init acc) resets ACC at the start of a path; (step acc j state pay) sees the full STATE at grid index J (0 included) and returns true to continue, NIL to stop the path; (funcall pay amount k) records AMOUNT paid on payment date K (PATH-PAYMENT-DATES); (final acc state) the amount paid at expiry, if not stopped.

    PROCESS is resolved; read latent components through STATE-INDEX here, once. GRID is the simulation's TIME-GRID. The default for path-independent payoffs wraps PAYOFF-KERNEL: STEP does nothing and FINAL reads the observable.

  • [function] FINCL:STATE-INDEX PROCESS DESIGNATOR
