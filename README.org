* fincl: Quantitative Finance in Common Lisp
Currently, this library implements pricing of a handful of path-independent options, but is written in such a way that addition of arbitrary stochastic processes and new option payoff/exercise types is scalable.

Addition of other instruments such as bonds, swaps, CDs, etc. is planned.
* Examples
The blocks below form one session, run in order in a fresh image (=run-readme-check= in =tests/readme.lisp= does exactly that). Run Lisp inside the Nix dev shell (=nix develop=), which provides SBCL, the native libraries and the ASDF source registry.
** Setting up
#+begin_src lisp
(ql:quickload :fincl)

(defpackage #:fincl-session (:use #:cl #:fincl))
(in-package #:fincl-session)

;;; #D"2027-06-15" (dates) and #T"3M" (tenors) live in this named readtable.
(named-readtables:in-readtable fincl:syntax)

;;; Monte Carlo and parallel calibration run on an lparallel kernel.
(setf lparallel:*kernel* (lparallel:make-kernel 4))
#+end_src
** Pricing Options
Like QuantLib, pricing options in =fincl= requires 3 components: an option, a market (an observation of spot price and date), and a choice of pricing engine.
Depending on the type of option, the pricing engines can be analytic or approximations thereof. Any and all analytic pricing engines assume the stochastic dynamics of the underlying asset under which they were derived (e.g. geometric Brownian motion for Black-Scholes, any process with characteristic function for Fourier cosine).

Rates, vols and dividends are decimals (=0.2d0= is 20%), times are years in the market's day count (ACT/365F by default), and =price= returns =(values npv standard-error engine)=; the standard error is NIL for deterministic engines.
#+begin_src lisp
(defparameter *today* #D"2026-10-01")

(defparameter *market*
  (make-market :valuation-date *today*
               :underlyings '((:foo :spot 100 :vol 0.2d0 :dividend 0.012d0 :currency :usd))
               :curves '((:usd-ois :rate 0.0425d0))))

(defparameter *euro-call* (make-option (call 110) (expires #T"1Y" :from *today*)))
(defparameter *amer-put* (make-option (put 80) (american) (expires #T"1Y" :from *today*)))

;;; Classical Black-Scholes formula
(price *euro-call* :black-scholes *market*)

;;; Let fincl choose: the closed form for a European under GBM
(price *euro-call* :auto *market*)

;;; Barone-Adesi & Whaley approximation of Americans, and the binomial tree
;;; that automatic selection prefers
(price *amer-put* :baw *market*)
(price *amer-put* :auto *market*)

;;; Monte Carlo with Heston dynamics; parameters left out come from the market
(price *euro-call*
       (make-engine :mc
                    :process (make-heston :xi 0.5d0)
                    :n-paths 50000)
       ,*market*)

;;; Greeks (vega per unit vol, theta per calendar day) and Black implied vol
(greeks *euro-call*
        :black-scholes *market*
        :which '(:delta :gamma :vega :theta))
(implied-volatility *euro-call* 5d0 :black-scholes *market*)
#+end_src

Black-Scholes and Barone-Adesi-Whaley price with the market's own vol. To price with another vol, give it to a model through an engine that takes a process, or move the market's vol:
#+begin_src lisp
;;; Pricing via Fourier-cosine
(price *euro-call* (make-engine :cos :process (make-gbm :vol 0.3d0)) *market*)
;;; Price via BS with +0.1 volatility of underlying
(price *euro-call* :black-scholes (bump *market* '(:vol :foo) '(:absolute 0.1d0)))
#+end_src
** Lispy-ness and Extensibility
=fincl= offers high level DSLs for creation of certain objects. One such example is options.
To create an option, you can call =make-option= and pass various clauses specifying different properties of the option.
#+begin_src lisp
;;; Vanilla European call K = 110. Explicit expiry
(make-option (call 110) (expires #D"2027-10-01"))

;;; Vanilla American put K = 95. Tenor expiry
(make-option (american) (put 95) (expires #T"1Y" :from *today*))

;;; Bermudan. The last exercise date is the expiry
(make-option (put 95) (bermudan :dates (list #D"2027-01-04" #D"2027-04-01" #D"2027-07-01")))
#+end_src

=fincl= attempts to offer the extensibility of common lisp to the user with macros.
Currently, a user can define arbitrary path-independent payoff schemes for an instrument, which the Monte Carlo engine can price under any of its processes.

For example, suppose we wanted to make a toy option payoff scheme that pays 1 unit of currency if the price of the underlying is beyond the strike at time of exercise (above it for a call, below it for a put). This of course is just a binary option.
The macro =define-payoff= can be used to define such a payoff scheme. It builds on =strike-payoff=, a payoff with a kind (call or put, which sets =phi=, a call-put adjusting coefficient) and a strike, but no payoff formula of its own.
The user supplies the payoff function at definition:
#+begin_src lisp
(define-payoff binary (strike-payoff)
    nil
    ;;; data needed for payoff kernel
    (spot)
  ;;; logic in payoff: pays 1 beyond the strike
  (if (plusp (* phi (- spot strike))) 1d0 0d0))

;;; Every payoff defined this way is also a clause of MAKE-OPTION
(defparameter *binary* (make-option (call 110) (binary) (expires #T"1Y" :from *today*)))
#+end_src

Pricing Europeans and Americans with Monte Carlo (respectively, Longstaff-Schwartz) with arbitrary underlying stochastic dynamics is now automatic:
#+begin_src lisp
(price *binary* (make-engine :mc :n-paths 100000) *market*)
(price (make-option
        (call 110)
        (binary)
        (american)
        (expires #T"1Y" :from *today*))
       (make-engine :mc :n-paths 50000 :process (make-heston :xi 0.5d0))
       ,*market*)
#+end_src
Of course, no analytic methods can be made available given the information =fincl= knows: the closed forms decline the payoff, and automatic selection falls back to Monte Carlo with an =engine-fallback= warning.
#+begin_src lisp
(handler-case (price *binary* :black-scholes *market*)
  (unsupported-combination (c) (princ-to-string c)))
#+end_src

Addition of path-dependent payoffs (such as those that resemble Asian options) is planned.
