#+title: Markets, curves, and dates

Use the package/readtable setup in [[./getting-started.org]] first.

* Named market data
A composite market groups data by name. Currency selects discounting;
the underlying selects spot, dividends, and equity vol.
#+begin_src lisp
(defparameter *named-market*
  (make-market
   :valuation-date #D"2026-10-01"
   :underlyings '((:foo :spot 100 :currency :usd
                         :dividend 0.01d0 :vol 0.2d0))
   :curves '((:usd-ois :rate 0.04d0))
   :discounting '((:usd . :usd-ois))))
(defparameter *stock-view* (market-view *named-market* :underlying :foo))

(spot *stock-view*)
;; => 100.0d0
(discount-factor *stock-view* #D"2027-10-01")
;; => 0.9607894391523232d0 (96.07894391523232d0%)
(forward *stock-view* #D"2027-10-01")
;; => 103.0454533953517d0
#+end_src


The discount factor is exp(-0.04); the forward is 100 * exp(0.03).
With several underlyings, put =(underlying :stock)= in the option clauses.
Currency conversion and quanto pricing are not supplied by this view.

* A dated curve
Discount factors at dates can replace a flat rate. The first date is the
reference date and its discount factor is one. The default interpolation
below is log-linear in discount factors; beyond the last knot the curve
extends its last instantaneous forward rate.
#+begin_src lisp
(defparameter *usd-curve*
  (make-instance 'interpolated-discount-curve
                 :dates (list #D"2026-10-01" #D"2027-10-01" #D"2028-10-01")
                 :discounts '(1d0 0.96d0 0.92d0)
                 :day-counter +actual-365-fixed+))
(curve-discount *usd-curve* #D"2027-10-01")
;; => 0.96d0

(make-market :valuation-date #D"2026-10-01"
             :curves `((:usd-ois :curve ,*usd-curve*))
             :discounting '((:usd . :usd-ois)))
#+end_src
INTERPOLATED-ZERO-CURVE and INTERPOLATED-FORWARD-CURVE store zero and
instantaneous forward rates instead. BOOTSTRAP-CURVE fits helper quotes;
for example, a deposit helper specifies a quote, index, and valuation date.
#+begin_src lisp
(defparameter *deposit-curve*
  (bootstrap-curve
   (list (deposit-helper 0.025d0 (euribor #T"3M")
                         :valuation-date #D"2026-10-01")
         (deposit-helper 0.027d0 (euribor #T"6M")
                         :valuation-date #D"2026-10-01"))))
#+end_src

* A rate with conventions
#+begin_src lisp
(defparameter *quoted-rate*
  (make-interest-rate 0.05d0 :day-counter +actual-360+
                             :compounding :compounded
                             :frequency :semiannual))
(compound-factor *quoted-rate* #D"2026-10-01" #D"2027-10-01")
;; => 1.0513458770904074d0 (105.13458770904074d0%)
#+end_src
Coupon accrual, curve time, and a yield quote need not share a day count.
Supply the relevant convention rather than reusing the market's by accident.

* Scenarios and dates
BUMP returns another market. A relative spot shift of 0.01 is a 1% move;
an absolute vol shift of 0.01 is one volatility percentage point.
#+begin_src lisp
(bump *named-market* '(:spot :foo) '(:relative 0.01d0))
(bump *named-market* '(:vol :foo) '(:absolute 0.01d0))
(bump *named-market* '(:curve :usd-ois) '(:absolute 0.0001d0))
(derive-market *named-market* :valuation-date #D"2026-10-02")

(adjust +target+ #D"2026-10-03" :following) ; Monday 2026-10-05
(make-schedule #D"2027-10-01" #D"2029-10-01" #T"6M" :calendar +target+)
;; => #<SCHEDULE #D"2027-10-01" to #D"2029-10-01", 4 periods>
#+end_src
A curve bump shifts zero-rate knots for interpolated curves and helper
quotes for bootstrapped curves. These are different risk experiments.
DERIVE-MARKET changes the observation date/convention, not the observed
quotes or spot. It is an aging scenario, not a forecast of tomorrow's market.

See [[./rates.org]] for separate forecasting/discounting and historical fixings.
