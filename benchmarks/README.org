* Overview
These results were generated by replaying the saved run, =benchmarks/results/2026-10-21T171828Z.sexp= (not provided in repo), with
=(fincl/benchmarks:replay-results "benchmarks/results/2026-10-21T171828Z.sexp" :output :org)=. No benchmark was run to produce this page.
You can produce the benchmarks on your system by loading =fincl/benchmarks= and running =fincl/benchmarks:run-benchmarks=.

How to read the tables:
- =ratio= is fincl's median time over QuantLib's: above 1 means fincl is slower. The geometric mean is taken within a benchmark group
- =per path= and =per step= divide the median by the paths and steps of the row. =cost to 1e-3 se= is the time needed to reach a standard error of 1e-3, so engines with different variance compare fairly.
- =verdict= compares the two values. =DIFFER= is not a failure to accurately price: it means the two libraries disagree by more than their method's tolerance, and the notes say which side is closer to a known reference.
- QuantLib is not the ground truth. Where a closed form, a converged tree or a converged COS value exists, the notes compare both libraries against it.

Each section states the quant task it benchmarks, shows the table generated from the run, and explains the differences in its notes. Times are medians over 30 samples (fewer for slow calls), on one thread unless a row says otherwise.

* Summary
- *Monte Carlo per path.* fincl is faster than QuantLib on every path-simulation row (2 to 7 times per path on one thread), and its per-path cost is within 2.5 times of a hand-written C++ loop.
- *Trees and closed-form barriers.* fincl is faster on the plain CRR tree, the continuous barriers and Merton. Its BBSR tree is more accurate than QuantLib's plain tree at the same size.
- *Finite differences.* fincl's European and American values are closer to their references than QuantLib's; the Bermudan and barrier values agree with theirs within 1e-4, with QuantLib marginally closer on the Bermudan. fincl is 1.1 to 2.2 times slower.
- *Characteristic-function prices (COS, Heston, Bates, VG).* Prices agree to about 1e-8 or better, and fincl is 1.5 to 4 times slower per call.
- *Rates and calibration.* fincl is slower, by 1 to 6 times on instruments and 6 to 630 times on the calibration fits; the largest gaps are the CEV numerics and the SVI reduction.

** Notes
- *European call, Black-Scholes.* Both give 9.2270055. fincl takes 3.4 us against QuantLib's 5.2 us; the closed form is cheap on both sides, so most of the time is per-call overhead.
- *American put, Barone-Adesi-Whaley.* Both agree to 4e-10, and fincl is 6.3 times slower. I have not profiled it; the approximation's critical-price search is the first place I would look.
- *American put, plain CRR, 1000 steps.* Both are the same tree and agree to 1e-5, and fincl is 3.7 times faster. A plausible cause is that the lattice in fincl is typed double-float arithmetic while QuantLib works through generic lattice objects
- *American put, fincl's default tree (BBSR).* Its value, 6.66074, is closer to the 10 000-step reference (6.66069) than QuantLib's plain tree at the same 1000 steps (6.65977). Smoothing the last step and extrapolating in N costs about 2.6 times the plain tree and buys accuracy. QuantLib is the less accurate of the two here.
- *Bermudan put, quarterly exercise.* The tree rows agree (6.56591 and 6.56590) and the speeds are close (1.07 times). The COS value, 6.56703, is the reference: the 1000-step tree is 1.1e-3 low, so the tree's discretisation error is the difference, not COS.
- *Heston European, analytic.* Not a like-for-like comparison: QuantLib integrates the characteristic function at 128 Gauss-Laguerre points, fincl sums 256 cosine terms. The matched row is the next one.
- *Heston European, COS on both sides.* Both prices are 9.0595069, and fincl is 2.2 times slower. Each price evaluates the characteristic function 256 times with complex arithmetic, so the cost is per term. Typing the characteristic function (task 28) halved the time and cut consing from 644 KB to 154 KB.
- *Bates, Merton and variance gamma.* Bates is 3.7 times slower, the same per-term cost as Heston plus the jump factor. Merton is 3.4 times faster, although fincl's Poisson series is truncated more tightly (weight 1e-16 against QuantLib's 1e-12 relative accuracy); the cause is not profiled. Variance gamma is 1.5 times slower; QuantLib integrates the conditional Black price over the gamma density and fincl uses COS. Prices agree to 1e-11 or better.
- *CEV, 276 times slower.* QuantLib uses a driftless CEV process, but the prices agree to 3e-14, so the drift convention is not the explanation: with r = q the two models are identical, and the rows agree.  This is a performance issue in fincl.
- *Kou.* fincl only: QuantLib has no Kou engine in this build.

- *American put, Longstaff-Schwartz.* Both price within 0.4 standard errors of the 10 000-step tree reference (6.66069). fincl prices on fresh paths, which is low-biased, and QuantLib uses its own estimator; the difference between them is within noise.

** Notes
- *The value column is not a price.* For Heston, SABR and SVI it is the RMSE of the fit, and for Hull-White it is the fitted mean reversion. Both sides' values are essentially zero for the fits, so the verdict is "within tolerance".
- *Both recover the generating parameters.* Heston returns v0 0.05, kappa 1.5, theta 0.06, xi 0.6 and rho -0.65 in both libraries, as do SABR (alpha 2, rho -0.4, nu 0.6), SVI (a 0.03, b 0.1, rho -0.4, m 0.02, sigma 0.15) and Hull-White (sigma 0.009, with a 0.08 in both). Recovery is the test; speed is secondary.
- *Heston, 9 times slower.* fincl takes 78 Levenberg-Marquardt iterations to QuantLib's 38 evaluations, and each iteration reprices the whole grid with COS, so the per-price cost in options-pricing is multiplied. On four workers fincl is 3.7 times slower. QuantLib's fit reaches an RMSE of 8e-14 and fincl's 6e-10; both are effectively exact.
- *SVI, 630 times slower.* fincl runs Zeletskii and De Marco's reduction (a Nelder-Mead search with a constrained least-squares step at each evaluation) and then a Levenberg-Marquardt. QuantLib runs Levenberg-Marquardt alone from a fixed start. fincl's method is more robust to a poor starting point, so this is a different algorithm rather than a like-for-like speed comparison.
- *SABR, 12 times slower.* The same kind of difference: fincl starts from its own at-the-money guess and QuantLib from a fixed point with no restarts.
- *Hull-White, 6 times slower.* Both recover sigma 0.009. QuantLib stops at its own tolerance, leaving a maximum relative price error of 1.2e-7, against fincl's 8e-12. Here fincl is both slower and more strict.
